Day 1 · Morning · Orientation

CMMC Orientation and the Assessor Mindset

CMMC is not a cybersecurity test. It is a conformity regime, and you are training to be the person whose determination other people rely on without knowing you. Before the first requirement, understand what the program is, who governs which part of it, what the credential pathway actually requires, and the change of role that everything else in this week depends on.

Day 1 — AM ~3 hours No prerequisites Sets up all 5 days
⚠️ Regulatory status as of 16 November 2026. Check again before you teach.
  • 32 CFR Part 170 has been in force since 16 December 2024 and has not been amended.
  • The DFARS CMMC rule took effect 10 November 2025, beginning Phase 1 (Level 1 and Level 2 self-assessments).
  • Phase 2 (Level 2 C3PAO certification, due 10 November 2026) was suspended by the Department of War (DoD) on 13 July 2026. DFARS Class Deviation 2026-O0025 (Rev. 3, 3 September 2026) applies the suspension, and no restart date has been announced.
  • Still in force: NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2, DFARS 252.204-7012, SPRS self-assessments, and annual affirmations.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to…

  • State what CMMC is, what it governs, and what makes it different in kind from the frameworks you already know. Bloom: Understand
  • Name every body in the CMMC ecosystem and say which one governs organizations and which governs individuals. Bloom: Remember
  • Separate the assessor role from the consultant role, and name the moments where the line is easiest to cross. Bloom: Analyze
  • Apply the three-part standard — requirement, authority, evidence — to a claim about a contractor. Bloom: Apply
  • Map the CCP and CCA prerequisite chains against your own position today. Bloom: Create

1

What this workshop is, and what it is not

Five days, ten sessions, one company. You will scope it, plan the engagement, request and triage its evidence, render determinations against the fourteen NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 families, compute and defend its DoD Assessment score, run a two-day mock assessment against it, write the findings, and close it out with a plan of action. On the final afternoon you will be handed a company you have never seen and given thirty minutes.

This is preparation for the ISACA CCP and CCA credentials, and it is preparation for the work those credentials describe. Those are not the same thing, and where they diverge this workshop follows the work. An exam-only course teaches you the model. This one teaches you to render a determination you can defend when the company that dislikes it pushes back.

What it is not

  • Not a course in how to become compliant. You will spend five days finding problems in a company and never once being permitted to fix one. That constraint is the subject, not an inconvenience.
  • Not a technical security course. You are expected to know what a domain controller and a Conditional Access policy are. You are not expected to configure either.
  • Not a substitute for the primary sources. Everything here is traced to 32 CFR Part 170, the FAR, the DFARS, the NIST publications, the CAP, and the NARA CUI Registry. When this material and a governing document disagree, the governing document wins and this material is wrong.
Exam tip

The single most common source of wrong answers in CMMC study is confidently remembered material that was true two years ago. Two examples arrive in this workshop within the first day: who issues the CCP and CCA, and how many requirements CMMC Level 1 comprises. Both are widely mis-stated in circulating training decks. Cite the rule, not the deck.

2

Where CMMC sits among the frameworks you know

Most people arriving at CMMC already know a security framework, and the instinct is to file CMMC alongside it. That instinct produces one specific, predictable error later: the assumption that partial implementation earns partial credit. True in a maturity model. False in CMMC.

The differences that matter are not about content. CMMC Level 2 is NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 — the requirements are borrowed wholesale. What differs is status, enforcement, and how conformity is demonstrated.

FrameworkMandatory?Applies toHow conformity is shown
NIST CSF 2.0No — voluntaryAny organization Self-declared. No external verification exists or is expected.
NIST RMF (SP 800-37)Yes, for federal systemsFederal information systems under FISMA Authorization to Operate granted by a government Authorizing Official.
NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5Yes, for federal systemsFederal systems; underpins FedRAMP Security Assessment Report feeding an authorization decision.
ISO/IEC 27001No — but certifiableAny organization, by choice Certification by an accredited certification body.
CMMC Level 2Yes, when it is in the contractDIB contractors handling CUI Self-assessment or C3PAO certification, posted to SPRS, affirmed annually by a named official.
The distinction to carry forward

RMF and SP 800-53 are mandatory — but for federal agency systems, not for you as a contractor. CSF 2.0 and ISO 27001 are voluntary. CMMC is the only one of these that a contracting officer can make a condition of your company winning work. That is why it is assessed the way it is, and why an assessor here carries obligations an auditor of a voluntary framework does not.

3

Three levels, and what triggers each

The level is not chosen. It follows from the information the contractor will handle, and the contracting officer inserts the clause accordingly under 32 CFR Part 170.

LevelTriggered byRequirementsSourceHow assessed
Level 1Federal Contract Information15 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(i)–(xv), incorporated by 32 CFR 170.14(c)(2) Annual self-assessment and affirmation
Level 2Controlled Unclassified Information110 NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2Self-assessment or C3PAO certification, per the contract
Level 3CUI on the highest-priority programs110 plus a selected subset of NIST SP 800-172 32 CFR 170.14(c)(4)Government-led, by DIBCAC
Exam tip — the counting trap

Level 1 is 15 requirements. A great deal of circulating CMMC 2.0 material says 17, because earlier model documentation split FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(ix) into three separate practices. That split has no basis in the rule. 32 CFR 170.14(c)(2) incorporates fifteen paragraphs, and there are fifteen of them. Count them yourself.

Level 2 decomposes further, and this is the mechanic that decides most of your week. The 110 requirements break into roughly 320 assessment objectives in NIST SP 800-171A. Every objective under a requirement must be MET or NOT APPLICABLE for the parent requirement to be MET. There is no partial credit at the objective level, and an assessor who determines at the requirement level has not done the work.

4

The ecosystem — who governs what

The ecosystem has more bodies than the names suggest, and their responsibilities do not overlap the way people assume. Roughly one in twenty CCP questions is drawn from this table, and it is the most commonly mis-stated topic in published CMMC material.

Body or roleWhat it does
DoW CIO / CMMC PMOOwns the program. Issues the model, the assessment guides, and the scoping guides.
DIBCACThe government assessment arm. Conducts government-led assessments, including Level 3.
The Cyber ABThe accreditation body. Accredits C3PAOs under ISO/IEC 17020 and operates the CMMC Marketplace. Accredits organizations — not people.
ISACA, as CAICOThe certification body for individuals. Administers CCP, CCA, Lead CCA, and CCI. Took the role in December 2025; transition completed April 2026.
C3PAOAn accredited firm that conducts Level 2 certification assessments.
RPO / RPRegistered Provider Organizations and Registered Practitioners. Readiness consulting. They help you prepare; they may not assess you.
OSCOrganization Seeking Certification — the contractor being assessed.
CCP / CCAIndividually credentialed professionals. A CCA may serve on a certification assessment team; a CCP supports one.
The correction — expect this on the exam

The Cyber AB accredits organizations. ISACA, as CAICO, certifies individuals.

For years the Cyber AB did both, and an enormous amount of still-circulating material — training decks, blog posts, vendor guides, and some published course modules — says the Cyber AB issues the CCP and CCA. That has not been true since December 2025. If you take one fact out of this session, take this one.

5

The shift: consultant to assessor

An assessor is not a better consultant. The two roles answer different questions, serve different parties, and are held to different standards. Most students find this the hardest idea in the workshop, and it is not because it is complicated. It is because the rules prohibit things people are used to doing: helping, advising, fixing, recommending a vendor they trust.

ConsultantAssessor
Question answeredHow do we get better?What is true here?
ServesThe client’s improvementThe integrity of the determination
Success looks likeThe gap is closedThe finding is accurate and survives challenge
RelationshipCollaborative, ongoingProfessional, bounded, documented
On finding a problemPropose a fixRecord it, evidence it, and say nothing about the remedy
Governing documentThe engagement contractThe CoPC, the CAP, 32 CFR Part 170, and ISO/IEC 17020

The last row is the one that changes your working life. As an assessor you operate inside a conformity regime that exists precisely so that someone can rely on your determination without knowing you.

The line, in one sentence

A consultant is paid to make an organization better. An assessor is paid to say accurately what is true about it. You will feel the pull constantly — a company with obvious problems, staff who want help, an easy fix you could name in one sentence. Noticing the pull is fine. Acting on it disqualifies you.

6

The three-part standard

From this session onward, every claim you make in this workshop is held to three questions. Write them on something you will still have on Friday.

The questionWhat a weak answer looks like
RequirementWhat specifically is required? “They need to protect CUI.” Which requirement, and at which objective?
AuthorityWhat imposes it? “CMMC requires it.” CMMC is a program. Name the clause, the section, or the Registry category.
EvidenceWhat would demonstrate it, and would that be enough? “They have a policy.” A policy is a claim about intent, not proof of practice.

Days 1 and 2 exercise the first two properly and the third lightly. Day 3 afternoon onward is almost entirely the third, and it is the part that separates people who know CMMC from people who can assess against it.

8

Responsible use, and what never leaves the boundary

The CoPC's eighth guiding principle is Proper Use of Technology and AI, and it is not decorative. It exists because two things have become easy at the same time: pasting a client's system security plan into a chat window, and letting a tool draft a determination that is yours to make.

Two questions, every time

  • Is a tool making a determination that is mine to make? A model can summarize an artifact. It cannot render MET or NOT MET, because it cannot be held to the CoPC, cannot be challenged at an out-brief, and cannot sign anything. The determination is the credentialed human's, and the reasoning has to be theirs.
  • Is customer data leaving the boundary to reach that tool? An SSP extract, a Conditional Access export, a network diagram, an access control matrix, personnel names, CAGE codes, incident detail — all of it is confidential to the engagement and much of it describes exactly how to attack the OSC. Confidentiality is a separate CoPC principle and it does not have a convenience exception.
A real scenario, adjudicated on Day 2

To save time on a long evidence review, a team member pastes several paragraphs of the OSC's system security plan and a screenshot of its access-control configuration into a public web-based AI assistant, and asks it to draft finding language. They read the output, edit it, and enter it as their determination.

Two principles are engaged, not one, and the disposition is not “be careful next time.” You will rule on this scenario, cite the practices, and say who decides.

What this workshop asks of you

  • Nothing from the Meridian case needs protecting — it is fictional, and you may work it in any tool you like. Use the week to build the habit anyway, because the habit is what transfers.
  • Every claim you write carries its authority. If an AI tool supplied a citation, open the citation. Fabricated regulatory cites are common, confident, and career-ending in a findings report.
  • Disclose tool use where an engagement requires it, and never let a tool talk to the OSC.
9

The Meridian case

The thread begins · Day 1 Morning · Fictional case

Meet Meridian Precision Defense Systems, and read the solicitation

One company carries all five days. Meridian Precision Defense Systems, Inc. is a 147-person precision manufacturer and systems integrator, headquartered in Dayton, Ohio with a second site in Huntsville, Alabama. Founded 1998, employee-owned through an ESOP, roughly $31M in annual revenue, CAGE code 7RK42. It machines guidance system housings and actuator assemblies, and supports UAV subsystem integration.

It holds three active DoD contracts worth $17.9M, and all three carry DFARS 252.204-7012:

  • W58RGZ-23-C-0174 — Army AMCOM prime, firm fixed price, $11.4M. Actuator housing assemblies, technical data packages, qualification test reports. CUI//SP-CTI, ITAR-controlled drawings. Also carries 252.204-7019, -7020 and -7021.
  • RTX-SUB-2024-4489 — a $4.7M cost-plus subcontract under a Raytheon prime for AFLCMC. UAV airframe bracket design support, FEA reports, drawings to MIL-DTL-31000C. CUI//SP-CTI and limited CUI//PRVCY.
  • N68335-22-C-0552 — NAVAIR SBIR Phase II, $1.85M, in its final reports phase. Composite actuator prototype. CUI//SP-CTI and CUI//SP-EXPT.

Three contracts, three agencies, three different CUI category mixes, and one network carrying all of it. The company has a CISO, ten written policies, and a System Security Plan — which makes it a more realistic assessment subject than a company with nothing, and a harder one. Documents exist. Whether they describe the environment is the question you will spend the week answering.

Today the Army prime contract is up for recompete as solicitation W58RGZ-26-R-0301. Open the case workbench, read the Overview and Contracts tabs, and answer one question before Day 1 afternoon: what does this company have to be able to demonstrate in order to keep the contract that is 64% of its DoD backlog?

You are not determining the level yet. You are noticing that the solicitation never states one in plain language — and that somebody has to reason it out from what the contractor will actually receive.

For faculty

Faculty teaching segment

  • Signature assignment: Give students a one-page profile of a fictional defense supplier and the cover page of a fictional solicitation. They write a half-page memo: which CMMC level applies, which fact in the contract makes it so, and the three things they would need to know next.
  • Rubric criteria:
    • The level is tied to the information the contract involves (FCI or CUI), not to company size or reputation
    • Every claim names its source (32 CFR Part 170, the DFARS clause, or the contract text)
    • Fact, inference, and opinion are kept apart
    • No advice on how to fix anything; the memo describes, it does not consult
  • Lab idea: Have students explore the Meridian Case Workbench overview before class, then open with a two-minute "consultant or assessor?" sort of five statements.
  • Course fit: Intro to cybersecurity, security policy and compliance, GRC.
🎓 Teaching noteThe mindset shift is the whole lesson. When a student offers a fix, ask "who asked you?" and let the room answer.

Knowledge Check

Test your judgment

Ten questions in CCP style. The rationale is the point — several of the wrong answers are things you will hear stated confidently by working practitioners.

Q1.Which body certifies individuals as CCPs and CCAs?

Question 1 options
Correct: A. ISACA took the CMMC Assessor and Instructor Certification Organization role in December 2025, and the transition completed in April 2026. It administers CCP, CCA, Lead CCA, and CCI. Why the others fall short: B The Cyber AB is the accreditation body. It accredits C3PAOs under ISO/IEC 17020 and operates the Marketplace — organizations, not people. This was true historically, which is why so much circulating material still says it.; C The PMO owns the program and issues the model, assessment guides, and scoping guides. It does not certify individuals.; D DIBCAC is the government assessment arm and conducts government-led assessments, including Level 3. It has no credentialing role.

Q2.A contractor holds Federal Contract Information but no CUI. What CMMC level applies?

Question 2 options
Correct: B. FCI triggers FAR 52.204-21 and CMMC Level 1 — 15 requirements, annual self-assessment and affirmation. Why the others fall short: A There is no reduced-scope Level 2. Level 2 is triggered by CUI, and its requirement set is not scaled down by how little CUI is present.; C Level 2 is triggered by CUI. FCI alone does not reach it.; D A common and expensive misreading. FCI is squarely inside CMMC at Level 1; 32 CFR 170.14(c)(2) incorporates the fifteen FAR safeguarding requirements.

Q3.How many security requirements comprise CMMC Level 1?

Question 3 options
Correct: C. 32 CFR 170.14(c)(2) incorporates FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(i) through (xv). Fifteen paragraphs, fifteen requirements. Why the others fall short: A The most widely repeated wrong answer in CMMC study. Earlier CMMC 2.0 model documentation split FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(ix) into three practices. The split has no basis in the rule. If a deck says 17, the deck is out of date.; B 110 is Level 2, from NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2.; D Roughly 320 is the count of Level 2 assessment objectives in NIST SP 800-171A, not requirements, and not Level 1.

Q4.Under Level 2, a requirement has four assessment objectives. Three are MET and one is not. What is the determination for the requirement?

Question 4 options
Correct: D. Every assessment objective must be MET or NOT APPLICABLE for the parent requirement to be MET. One objective unmet makes the whole requirement NOT MET. There is no partial credit. Why the others fall short: A There is no such determination. A noted deficiency at objective level is exactly what makes the requirement NOT MET.; B Partially MET does not exist in CMMC. This is the single most consequential import from maturity-model thinking, and it produces determinations that cannot be defended.; C Point values belong to the DoD Assessment Methodology score, which is a different instrument. They have nothing to do with the MET / NOT MET determination.

Q5.An assessor reviewing evidence notices an obvious, cheap fix for a gap and mentions it to the IT Manager during the walkthrough. What has happened?

Question 5 options
Correct: C. An assessor records a problem, evidences it, and says nothing about the remedy. Naming a risk is assessment; naming its fix is not. Why the others fall short: A The prohibition is not about the report. It is about the role. Advice given verbally in a corridor is still advice, and it is still consulting.; B This is the reasoning that ends careers, and it feels helpful at the time. Adding value to the OSC is the consultant's job, and doing it inside an assessment destroys the independence the determination rests on.; D The OSC will ask. It will ask reasonably, warmly, and repeatedly. Being asked does not change the rule — and being able to decline without lecturing is a skill this workshop assesses.

Q6.Which of these is the correct three-part standard for a claim in an assessment finding?

Question 6 options
Correct: A. What specifically is required; what regulation, clause, or official document imposes it; and what would demonstrate it — and whether that would be enough. Why the others fall short: B That is a risk-assessment triad. It is not how a conformity determination is justified.; C A useful maturity heuristic, and not the standard for defending a determination. It names artifact types, not the burden of proof.; D Those are the three assessment methods from NIST SP 800-171A. They are how you gather evidence, not how you justify a claim.

Q7.Who may serve as an OSC's Affirming Official?

Question 7 options
Correct: D. And the exposure attaches personally: an affirmation that is not true is a false statement in a federal system, with False Claims Act consequences for the person who signed it. Why the others fall short: A Knowledge of the environment is not the criterion. Authority to bind the organization is.; B Never. The assessor does not affirm the organization's compliance; that would collapse the independence the whole regime rests on.; C A consultant cannot bind the OSC and cannot affirm on its behalf.

Q8.An assessment team member pastes extracts from the OSC's system security plan into a public AI assistant to draft finding language. Which CoPC principles are engaged?

Question 8 options
Correct: B. Two breaches, not one: engagement-confidential information left the boundary, and a tool was used to produce reasoning that is the credentialed assessor's to produce. Information Integrity is arguably a third. Why the others fall short: A It is the obvious one, and it is not the only one. Stopping here misses the more serious breach.; C Confidentiality is engaged, but so is the question of who is permitted to render the determination.; D Editing the output addresses neither problem. The data has already left, and the reasoning still did not originate with the assessor.

Q9.What is the relationship between CMMC Level 2 and NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2?

Question 9 options
Correct: D. The controls are borrowed wholesale. What CMMC adds is the assessment regime, the levels, the scoping rules, affirmation, and enforceability through a contract clause. Why the others fall short: A There is no subset at Level 2. All 110 apply.; B Rev 3 is not the assessment standard. CMMC Level 2 remains anchored to Rev 2; Rev 3 is horizon-scanning only until future rulemaking says otherwise.; C That describes Level 3, which layers a selected subset of 800-172 on top of the 110.

Q10.A Registered Practitioner (RP) helped an OSC prepare for a Level 1 self-assessment two years ago. The RP is now proposed for the OSC's Level 2 certification assessment team. What applies?

Question 10 options
Correct: C. 32 CFR 170.8(b)(17)(ii)(G), carried into CoPC 2.2(d), prohibits participating in the Level 2 certification process where the member consulted to prepare the organization for any CMMC assessment within three years. Why the others fall short: A The bar covers preparatory or consulting activity for any type of CMMC assessment. The level of the earlier work does not create an exemption.; B The prohibition runs three years, not two. And even outside it, other conflicts may still apply.; D The OSC's consent is irrelevant, and the individual does not decide their own eligibility. The C3PAO owns impartiality and conflict management, and that responsibility cannot be delegated.
