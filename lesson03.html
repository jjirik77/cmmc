Ethics, Independence, and Assessment Planning

This is where you stop being a practitioner. An assessor who is excellent at evaluating controls and careless about independence is worse than useless — the finding is unreliable, the certificate is unsafe, and the whole verification regime is worth less for their having participated in it. Most people find this the hardest session, and not because the material is difficult.

A

Learning objectives

By the end of this session you will be able to:

  • ApplyApply the eight guiding principles of the CMMC Code of Professional Conduct as decision rules to concrete engagement situations, rather than recite them.
  • IdentifyIdentify conflicts of interest and impartiality threats, and distinguish those that can be mitigated from those that must be avoided.
  • StateState the three-year consulting prohibition accurately — what it covers, what it does not, and who it binds.
  • ExecuteExecute the CAP preliminary proceedings and Phase 1 activities: framing, conflict management, scope validation, evidence confirmation, team composition, and the readiness determination.
  • ProduceProduce a defensible assessment plan with an independence declaration — including, where warranted, a determination that the engagement may not proceed.
B

Key terminology

Click a card to reveal the definition. These are the terms the CCP and CCA exams use precisely, and the terms practitioners most often use loosely.

1

The shift, stated plainly

For two sessions you have been learning what the requirements are. Now your judgment about them has consequences for another company.

That change carries obligations with nothing to do with technical skill. If you currently work as a consultant, some of what you do routinely is prohibited to you as an assessor, and it is better to confront that now than to discover it mid-engagement.

The distinction to carry into every remaining session

A consultant is paid to make an organization better. An assessor is paid to say accurately what is true about it. Those are different jobs, and doing the first inside the second destroys the value of both.

2

The eight principles, as decision rules

The CoPC lists eight guiding principles in Article II, each with practices beneath it. The practices are the operative text. Learn the principles as questions you ask yourself, not as a list you recite.

PrincipleThe question it asks
ProfessionalismAm I representing myself, my credentials, and my services accurately — and refraining from guarantees, low-balling, and disparagement?
ImpartialityIs there anything about my relationships, interests, or history that could compromise this determination, or reasonably appear to?
ConfidentialityIs this information leaving a boundary it was entrusted to me inside?
Information IntegrityIs what I am recording accurate, complete, and traceable to evidence?
Lawful and Ethical BehaviorDoes this comply with the law and the regulation, and not merely with the contract?
Equal OpportunityAm I applying the same standard I would apply to a larger, smaller, friendlier, or more difficult OSC?
Proper Use of CMMC MethodsAm I staying inside my role and using approved methods and materials — a CCP not encroaching on a CCA's responsibilities, for instance?
Proper Use of Technology and AIIs a tool making a determination that is mine to make — and is customer data leaving the boundary to reach that tool?
Exam tip

CoPC content is only about 5% of the CCP exam and produces a disproportionate share of the failures, precisely because candidates assume ethics is intuition and skip the document. “Impartiality” alone is not a citation; §2.2(g) is.

3

Impartiality and the three-year bar

Impartiality carries the most operative detail and ends the most careers. The CoPC is blunt about why: the ecosystem must operate free of undue commercial, personal, financial, or other pressures, because impartiality is the core of what makes a certificate mean anything.

State the prohibition precisely

32 CFR §170.8(b)(17)(ii)(G), carried into CoPC §2.2(d), prohibits ecosystem members from participating in the Level 2 certification process for an assessment in which they previously served as a consultant to prepare the organization for any type of CMMC assessment within three years.

It does coverIt does not say
Preparatory, advisory, or consulting activity of any kind, for any type of CMMC assessment That every prior relationship with an OSC is barred for three years
Consulting for a Level 1 self-assessment two years ago, which bars you from a Level 2 certification assessment today That the bar is the only conflict rule — most conflicts arise from other relationships entirely
The C3PAO as an organization, not only the individual assessors That being clear of the three-year bar means you are clear to proceed
Supplying templates, workbooks, or tools that guide implementation — this is advisory activity That you get to decide your own eligibility
The rule underneath the rule

The C3PAO owns impartiality and conflict management. The CAP says so twice, and says the responsibility cannot be delegated to the assessment team or to the OSC. Your obligation is to disclose, in writing, as soon as a conflict is known or reasonably should be known. The decision about whether it can be mitigated is not yours. Assessors get into trouble far more often for deciding a conflict was fine than for having one.

Mitigate or avoid

Some conflicts can be managed; some cannot. The CoPC's own worked examples set the pattern: a family relationship is mitigated by removing the assessor from the team, while a C3PAO assessing an environment shaped by its own products has no available mitigation and must decline entirely. If the conflict cannot be sufficiently mitigated, the C3PAO does not proceed.

4

What happens before Phase 1

An assessment does not begin with Phase 1. The CAP places preliminary proceedings ahead of it, and two of them decide whether there will be an assessment at all.

Preliminary activityWhat it settles
Receive the assessment requestThe OSC approaches an authorized or accredited C3PAO listed in the Marketplace. Neither the Cyber AB, the CAICO, nor DoD may recommend or introduce a C3PAO.
Confirm the entityThe precise legal entity and the CAGE code or codes. A certification attaches to an identified information system owned and operated by an identified OSC. No CAGE code, no assessment.
Frame the assessmentSchedule, size, personnel, logistics, contractual requirements, and the prospective scope, worked out with the Affirming Official or OSC point of contact.
Identify and manage conflictsThe C3PAO proposes the Lead CCA and coordinates with the OSC to establish whether conflicts exist. Documented mitigation plan required; where a conflict cannot be sufficiently mitigated, the C3PAO does not proceed.
Execute the contractual agreementA written contract and a mutual NDA. The contract may not include guarantees, promises of outcome, or any incentive or bonus contingent on a certificate being issued.
Two things students routinely get backwards

Conflicts are managed before Phase 1, not during it — and the responsibility sits with the C3PAO, which cannot delegate it to the assessment team or to the OSC.

A contract that pays a bonus on certification, or refunds a fee if certification is not achieved, is prohibited. If you see one in an engagement letter, that is a finding about the C3PAO, not a commercial detail.

5

Phase 1: Conduct the Pre-Assessment

Phase 1 asks one question: is this organization ready to be assessed? Not whether it will pass — the CAP is explicit that the team may not speculate about or intimate a likely outcome. Only whether the evaluative work of Phase 2 can sensibly begin.

Phase 1 activityWhat it involves
Review the SSPA cursory review for completeness, accuracy, and consistency — enough to form a reasonable expectation that the requirements have been addressed. Not an evaluation of adequacy or sufficiency; that is Phase 2.
Validate the scopeThe Lead CCA validates scope under 32 CFR §170.19(c). Disagreements about scope must be resolved before Phase 2 may begin.
Address ESPsConfirm a Customer Responsibility Matrix will be available and that ESP personnel will participate. If the ESP handles CUI, confirm the OSC can produce FedRAMP Moderate authorization, documented equivalency, or a Level 2 certificate.
Confirm evidence availabilitySatisfy yourself that enough evidence and enough people will actually be accessible to support accurate determinations.
Determine readinessThe Lead CCA makes the call and conveys which assessment methods will be used, at a depth and coverage drawn from NIST SP 800-171A and SP 800-53A.
Compose the teamThe C3PAO composes the team under 32 CFR §170.11(b)(10) and ISO/IEC 17020 personnel procedures, proposes names to the OSC, and manages conflicts before Phase 2 begins.
Complete the Pre-Assessment FormCAGE code, SSP title, contacts, team, dates, readiness determination. Not shared outside the engagement.
QA review, then uploadA C3PAO quality assurance individual reviews the form and uploads it into CMMC eMASS. Phase 1 concludes on that successful upload — not on a meeting.
And if the OSC is not ready

The Lead CCA may determine that it is not. That is a legitimate Phase 1 outcome and the paperwork is still completed. Under no circumstances does the C3PAO, the team, or anyone affiliated with it then help the OSC get ready — the consulting bar arriving at the moment it is hardest to honour.

6

Applied: the Meridian engagement

Marcus Ellison contacted Tidewater Assurance Group, LLC directly, requesting a Level 2 certification assessment. The AMCOM recompete is 18 months out and the company has never submitted an SPRS score. Fieldwork requested within 120 days; she has asked twice whether it can be sooner.

Why the engagement looks straightforwardWhat Phase 1 actually finds
There is a CISO who owns the SSP, the policies and the POA&MShe keeps the POA&M in a personal OneDrive outside the authorised CUI boundary, and it has not been AO-reviewed in 18 months.
A System Security Plan exists — version 2.1Dated October 2023 and marked “needs update” in the company's own register. It does not describe the MSP relationship, two CUI-handling suppliers, or the data flow into AWS GovCloud.
Ten written policies, four of them currentThe Incident Response Plan is three years stale and names a departed employee. Media Protection is four years stale. The Contingency Plan has been a 0.9 draft since it was written.
A network diagram exists, and it is accurateIt accurately depicts three subnets routing freely through one switch with no VLAN enforcement. The diagram is not the problem; what it shows is.
Eight gaps are already documented and ratedDocumented by whom — and what does that do to this engagement?
Cloud is done properly — GCC High and AWS GovCloud, both FedRAMP HighAnd the GCC High tenant excludes its most privileged account from the MFA policy. The procurement decision was right; the configuration was never finished.
147 people, one domain, two sites — a manageable environmentNo asset inventory exists. Scope cannot be validated against a list of assets that has never been written.

The readiness question is genuinely close

Meridian is a harder Phase 1 than a company with nothing, and harder in the direction that matters. The artifacts the CAP requires Phase 1 to review mostly exist. An SSP can be reviewed for completeness, accuracy and consistency. Policies can be read. A network diagram can be examined. On a checklist reading, this company is ready.

Phase 1 asks whether the evaluative work of Phase 2 can sensibly begin, and the answer turns on one thing the checklist has no line for. Scope cannot be validated under 32 CFR §170.19(c), because no asset inventory exists. The Lead CCA is required to validate scope; scope disagreements must be resolved before Phase 2 may begin; and there is no enumeration of assets against which to do either.

An assessor who reaches “not ready” here has to earn it with a specific reason rather than a general impression of disorder — which is exactly the discipline the determination demands.

The proposed team

  • Dana Whitfield — proposed Lead CCA. Eleven Level 2 assessments as Lead. No prior relationship with Meridian; did not participate in the gap assessment.
  • Marcus Reyes — CCA. Employed by Meridian 2019–2022, where he built the Active Directory group structure and the file server permission model still in use, including on the /CUI tree. Participated in the gap assessment.
  • Priya Raghunathan — CCP. Two years alongside Meridian's IT Manager on their local professional association chapter board; they see each other monthly. Participated in the gap assessment.
  • Thomas Blalock — CCA. Holds roughly $30,000 of stock in Cardinal Industrial Group, the parent company of ClearPath IT Solutions, the vendor with standing remote access to Meridian's PLM. Disclosed on joining.
  • Nadia Farhat — quality assurance. Not on the team; reviews and uploads the Pre-Assessment Form.

The engagement letter

Two terms are worth reading twice. An outcome provision: “In the event a Certificate of CMMC Status is not issued, thirty percent of the fee shall be credited against a subsequent assessment.” And a support provision: “Tidewater will make its assessors available to answer implementation questions arising from the assessment for a period of 60 days following the out-brief.”

And then the part that tests you

Ellison has asked whether Tidewater can “take a pass at the inventory and tidy up the SSP first, so we are not wasting your people's time.” It is a reasonable-sounding request from a company that has genuinely tried and is short of one artifact. Being able to say why the answer is no — without being prompted, and without lecturing him — is what this session has been building toward.

Meridian — your turn

Meridian · Day 2 Afternoon · Deliverable D3

Rule on the engagement, then plan it

You are now Tidewater. The Engagement & Ethics Gate puts the packet in front of you and asks the question Phase 1 exists to answer — twice.

First: may this engagement proceed at all? Work every conflict in the packet individually. For each: name the principle and the specific practice, decide disclose, mitigate, or avoid, and say who decides. Some are in the team biographies. At least one is not.

Then: on the stated assumption that a conflict-free team and organization have been substituted, build the plan — engagement scope, team composition, independence declaration, evidence request approach, interview schedule, and readiness risks.

The tool also runs the eight ethics scenarios, from the former employer through the drafted finding produced by a public AI assistant, and adjudicates each against the text.

No remediation advice anywhere, including in the readiness risks. Naming a risk is assessment; naming its fix is consulting. And a plan that quietly ignores a disqualifying conflict in order to have something to plan is the exact failure this session exists to prevent.
