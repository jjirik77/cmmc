The Threaded Case Study

Meridian Precision Defense Systems, Carried End to End

One fictional 147-person precision manufacturer in Dayton, Ohio, with a second site in Huntsville, Alabama, holding three DoD contracts worth $17.9M — an Army AMCOM prime, a Raytheon subcontract, and a NAVAIR SBIR. All three carry DFARS 252.204-7012. It has a CISO, ten written policies, a System Security Plan, an accurate network diagram, and cloud done properly in GCC High and AWS GovCloud. It also has three subnets routing freely through one switch, an MSP holding standing domain admin under a month-to-month agreement, six VPN accounts belonging to people who left, and no asset inventory at all. Every session closes with a segment of its assessment, executed in a tool rather than on paper. By Friday afternoon you have run a complete Level 2 assessment on it.

And then the honest test

Day 5 afternoon closes with Acuitas Machining — fourteen people, no server, no domain, a commercial cloud tenant that fails the FedRAMP question outright, and three CNC controllers that retain CUI for months. It is a sub-tier supplier inside Meridian's own supply chain, it is issued cold, and you get thirty minutes. Four days inside one company produces a real risk: learning Meridian rather than learning a method. This is the only instrument that tells the two apart.

About This Workshop

Built for the Work, Not Only the Exam

Who this is for

Compliance officers, IT and security leads at defense industrial base contractors, Registered Practitioners and RPO staff, and candidates for the ISACA CCP and CCA credentials. It assumes three or more years of IT or security experience and basic familiarity with the DFARS. It does not assume you have ever conducted an assessment.

How it differs from exam-only training

An exam-only course teaches you the model. This one teaches you to render a determination you can defend when the company that dislikes it pushes back. You will spend five days finding problems in a company and never once being permitted to fix one — that constraint is the subject, not an inconvenience.

How to use it

Work the sessions in order; each depends on the one before it, and the tools carry state forward. Complete the Meridian segment before moving on — the Day 5 mock assessment assumes you have. Do not read ahead to the Acuitas transfer test; a student who has seen it beforehand has taken a different test.

On sources

Requirements are stated from primary sources and cited to part, section, or clause: 32 CFR Part 170, 32 CFR Part 2002, FAR 52.204-21, the DFARS 252.204 clause family, NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 and SP 800-171A, the CMMC Assessment Process v2.0, the Code of Professional Conduct v2.0, and the NARA CUI Registry. Where a fact could not be verified — the CCP and CCA exam duration and passing scores — it is marked unverified rather than guessed.
