CUI Determination — Auditing Meridian’s Inventory

Six information types, six company classifications, and your determination on each. Cited to the NARA CUI Registry, or to the element of the definition the item fails.

6 items + boundaryNARA RegistryPrints as D2

How to work this

Six information types, with Meridian's own classification reproduced verbatim. Treat each as a claim under test, not as a starting point you inherit.

For every item you determine is CUI: the exact Registry category name, the marking, Basic or Specified, and the authority the Registry cites. For every item you determine is FCI or neither: the element of the definition it fails, and which definition you applied.

Then say what kind of error the company made where it is wrong — a category that does not exist, a category that exists but does not fit, an internal contradiction, or an item classified too low.

Before you start

Some of the company's calls are right. You are not looking for an organization that got everything wrong. You are looking for the specific places where a plausible-sounding label does not survive contact with the authority — and for the one place where the company has under-classified, which is the direction that costs it money.

One item will not resolve cleanly from the Registry. That is a real outcome. Say so, say what you checked, and say what would settle it.

Two definitions, for reference

FCI — FAR 52.204-21(a). Information, not intended for public release, provided by or generated for the Government under a contract to develop or deliver a product or service. Excludes public information and simple transactional information such as that necessary to process payments.

CUI — 32 CFR 2002.4. Information the Government creates or possesses, or that an entity creates or possesses for or on behalf of the Government, that a law, regulation, or government-wide policy requires or permits an agency to handle with safeguarding or dissemination controls. Excludes classified information and information a company develops purely at its own expense for its own purposes.

1

Actuator housing technical data packages

Engineering drawings, dimensional tolerances, material specifications and qualification test protocols furnished by AMCOM after award under W58RGZ-23-C-0174. Portions bear Distribution Statement D and are ITAR-controlled. Held on SYS-003 and in the Arena PLM.

The company’s classification

CUI — “ITAR Data”

Model answer

CUI — and the company is right about that. Controlled Technical Information · CTI · Specified · banner CUI//SP-CTI. The Registry cites 48 CFR 252.204-7012; distribution statements under DoDI 5230.24; “technical data” as defined at DFARS 252.227-7013.

The error is the citation, not the determination. “ITAR Data” is not a Registry category name. ITAR is a control regime under 22 CFR Parts 120–130 that may apply to the same information; it is not the CUI category, and the two answer different questions. This is the most common real-world pattern in aerospace and ordnance work: people know the material is controlled, and reach for the regime they hear about most rather than the Registry.

Ask what a document containing this data must be marked, and who applies the marking if AMCOM failed to.

Error type: Invented category name.

2

UAV airframe bracket design files

FEA reports and engineering drawings to MIL-DTL-31000C, produced by Meridian under the Raytheon subcontract RTX-SUB-2024-4489 and delivered to the prime. Held in the PLM and the GCC High tenant.

The company’s classification

CUI — “CTI”

Model answer

CUI, and correct. Controlled Technical Information · CTI · Specified.

Note what makes it correct, because it is the part contractors get wrong. Meridian generated this material itself — it was not furnished by the Government. It is still CUI, because the definition at 32 CFR 2002.4 reaches information an entity creates for or on behalf of the Government, and because DFARS 252.204-7012 defines covered defense information to include information the contractor collects, develops, receives, transmits, uses or stores in support of contract performance.

Mark this one right and say why. An inventory in which every correct call is an accident is not an inventory you can rely on for the rest.

Error type: Correct.

3

Personnel security submissions

SF-86 data and DISS records for staff requiring a determination, collected on behalf of the Government. Held by Gregory Farris, FSO, in a restricted SharePoint library in the GCC High tenant.

The company’s classification

CUI — “Privacy—Personnel”

Model answer

CUI, and the company is right in substance. Privacy index: Personnel Records · PERS, and/or General Privacy · PRVCY. This material is collected and held for the Government under a personnel security process, which is what puts it inside the CUI definition.

Pair this deliberately with item 5. Both concern employees; one is CUI and one is not. The distinction is not sensitivity — it is who the information was created for. SF-86 and DISS material is collected on the Government's behalf; a W-4 and an ESOP participant statement are not.

Error type: Label is not a Registry name; substance correct.

4

SBIR final technical report and composite actuator research data

Research output under NAVAIR SBIR Phase II N68335-22-C-0552, now in its final reports phase. The contract identifies CUI//SP-EXPT. Analysis data is held in AWS GovCloud; the draft report is on SYS-003.

The company’s classification

“Proprietary R&D — company confidential”

Model answer

CUI. Export Controlled · EXPT, and the contract itself identifies CUI//SP-EXPT. Read the category page for whether the authority relied on makes it Specified or Basic — that is the point of the item.

The error is a category error, not a citation error. Meridian has classified work it performed as its own commercial property. But the CUI definition expressly reaches information an entity creates or possesses for or on behalf of the Government, and this was performed under a $1.85M federal contract that names the category.

SBIR data rights are real, and they are a different question. A contractor may hold data rights in technical data and that information may still be CUI. Rights govern what the Government may do with it; CUI status governs how it must be protected. Conflating the two is the specific error here, and it is extremely common in research-performing contractors.

An assessor who says “this is CUI//SP-EXPT under the contract, and your data rights position is unaffected by that” has made the determination and defused the argument in one sentence.

Error type: Treated as company property when the contract says otherwise.

5

Payroll, ESOP participant records, and commercial supplier invoices

Payroll and benefits for all 147 employees, ESOP participant statements, and invoices from commercial raw-material suppliers. Collected and generated by Meridian as an employer and as a business, at its own expense. Payroll and accounting in QuickBooks, read-only access held by an external CPA.

The company’s classification

Inconsistent — two company records disagree

Model answer

Neither CUI nor FCI. Fails 32 CFR 2002.4 — created and held by Meridian as an employer and a business, at its own expense, for its own purposes. Fails FCI at FAR 52.204-21(a) — not provided by or generated for the Government under any of the three contracts.

Resolve the contradiction; do not average it. The company's data classification page says this material is sensitive business data outside the CUI definition — that page is right. Its third-party register records Whitmore & Associates as handling “employee PII / CUI-Privacy,” while stating elsewhere that the firm is “not in SSP scope” — those entries are wrong and mutually inconsistent.

Neither page is authority; the Registry and the definition are. Expect resistance on this item: it is personal, it is regulated, and it is obviously sensitive. All true, none of it relevant. Ask what law requires an agency to control it.

Error type: Internal contradiction.

6

Cost and pricing data under the Raytheon subcontract

Indirect cost pools, negotiated labour rates, cost proposals and the supporting build-ups behind them, submitted to the prime under the CPFF subcontract and flowed to AFLCMC. Held on SYS-003 and in email.

The company’s classification

FCI

Model answer

Arguably CUI, and at minimum not settled. General Procurement and Acquisition · PROCURE · Specified · CUI//SP-PROCURE covers cost or pricing data, contract information, indirect costs and direct labour rates on its face. Authorities: 48 CFR 3.104-4; 41 U.S.C. 2105; 48 CFR 3.104-8; 48 CFR 52.215-1(e). Pre-award proposal evaluation material held by the Government would instead be Source Selection · SSEL.

This is the item classified too low, and it is the direction that costs money. Everything else in this inventory is either correct or over-reaching; this reverses it. A cost-plus subcontract generates far more cost and pricing material than a firm fixed price one, which is why the exposure sits here rather than on the larger Army contract.

If the company is wrong, material on SYS-003 and in email has been handled at the FCI standard when it should have been handled at the CUI standard — which changes the boundary and possibly the level analysis.

The argument is not airtight, and saying so is the stronger answer. What would settle it: the subcontract terms, any markings Raytheon applied, and ultimately the contracting officer.

Error type: Classified too low.

7

The determination that follows: level, boundary, and what you tell Priya Mehta

Three contracts are in play and all three carry DFARS 252.204-7012. State the level obligation and where the CUI boundary falls — naming systems, because “the network” is not a boundary.

Tick each system or party that is inside the CUI boundary

Model answer

Level. CMMC Level 2 under DFARS 252.204-7021 for the Army prime contract, which carries the clause expressly along with -7019 and -7020. The Raytheon subcontract and the NAVAIR SBIR both carry 252.204-7012 and both involve CUI, so the safeguarding obligation attaches across all three regardless of which one carries the CMMC clause. FAR 52.204-21 continues to apply wherever FCI is handled and is subsumed, not replaced.

Authority chain: 32 CFR 170.14 for the level structure; DFARS 252.204-7012 for the safeguarding obligation already in force on all three; DFARS 252.204-7019/-7020 for the SPRS assessment of record.

Inside: SYS-001 and SYS-002 (domain controllers), SYS-003 (file server, primary CUI store), SYS-004 (PLM), SYS-005 ×14 and SYS-006 ×6 (engineering workstations), SYS-007 (GCC High tenant), SYS-008 (AWS GovCloud), SYS-009/010 (firewall and VPN), SYS-011 (IDS), SYS-012 (MFP), the core switch, the CORP-LAN workstations, and the Huntsville network equipment. The absence of VLAN enforcement between CORP-LAN and ENG-LAN pulls in everything that can reach the CUI stores.

Outside: Whitmore & Associates as a service, and FastenerTech. Little else with confidence.

What separates Competent from Assessor-Ready: saying that the boundary is wide because of two specific absences — no enforced segmentation and no asset inventory — rather than because the company is generally disorganised. Meridian has an SSP, ten policies and an accurate network diagram. Naming exactly which missing artifact closes the conditional categories is the difference.

And saying what would change each answer: what the subcontract terms would have to say for item 6 to resolve; what Microsoft would have to commit to beyond the FedRAMP authorisation; and what evidence would move the Huntsville workstations across the line.

Third parties: ClearPath requires flow-down under 252.204-7012(m) and ESP treatment; Acuitas, AeroComp and Secure Shred all receive or take custody of CUI and none has an adequate agreement; the GCC High and GovCloud tenants are CSP questions under (b)(2)(ii)(D). The full ESP analysis belongs to Day 3 — naming the deferral is a strength, not a gap.
