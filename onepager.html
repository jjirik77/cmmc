CMMC (CCP) — Exam-Day One-Pager

The highest-yield rules from the workshop on one page: the assessor’s standard, evidence, scoping, scoring, POA&M limits, and the trap answers. Print it and review it the morning of the exam.

The assessor’s standard

  1. Requirement. What, specifically, is required?
  2. Authority. Which regulation, clause, or official document imposes it?
  3. Evidence. What would show it is met, and would that be enough? Every verdict says what would change it.
  4. Assess, never advise. The assessor judges what is there. Fixing it is the organization’s job.

Regulatory status as of 16 November 2026 — check again before you teach

  • 32 CFR Part 170 has been in force since 16 December 2024 and has not been amended.
  • The DFARS CMMC rule took effect 10 November 2025, beginning program Phase 1 (Level 1 and Level 2 self-assessments).
  • Program Phase 2 (Level 2 C3PAO certification, due 10 November 2026) was suspended by the Department of War (DoD) on 13 July 2026. DFARS Class Deviation 2026-O0025 (Rev. 3, 3 September 2026) applies the suspension. No restart date has been announced. November 2028 is the original Phase 4 date, not a restart date.
  • Still in force: NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2, DFARS 252.204-7012, SPRS self-assessments, and annual affirmations.
  • Do not confuse program Phase 2 (a rollout milestone) with CAP Phase 2 (conducting an assessment).

Evidence

Adequate: the evidence exists and matches what the requirement intends (a property of the artifact). Sufficient: it shows the control is implemented, followed, and operating across the scope, now. A verbal assurance is neither.

Examine shows that something exists and what it says, not that anyone follows it. Interview gives leads, never conclusions. Test shows it operates now, not last quarter. Sampling is nonstatistical, with depth and coverage increased when evidence is questionable.

Determinations and scoring

MET / NOT MET / NOT APPLICABLE at the assessment-objective level: 110 requirements, about 320 objectives. One objective unmet makes the requirement NOT MET. N/A scores as MET, so expect it to be challenged.

DoD Assessment Methodology: start at 110, deduct 5 / 3 / 1, floor −203. Partial credit exists for exactly two requirements:

RequirementDeduct 3Deduct 5
3.5.3 MFARemote and privileged users covered, general users notNo MFA
3.13.11 FIPSEncryption used, not FIPS validatedNo encryption

POA&M — 32 CFR §170.21

Threshold: score ÷ 110 ≥ 0.8 (88 points). These may never be on a POA&M: AC.L2-3.1.20 AC.L2-3.1.22 CA.L2-3.12.4 PE.L2-3.10.3 PE.L2-3.10.4 PE.L2-3.10.5. Two are worth one point each, so the score alone never answers the question. SC.L2-3.13.11 may be deferred only where encryption is used but not FIPS validated. Closeout window: 180 days from the Conditional status date.

Incident reporting — DFARS 252.204-7012

72 hours from discovery, through DIBNet, with a DoD-approved medium assurance certificate. Threshold: potentially adverse. Preserve images and monitoring data 90 days. Malicious software goes to DC3, not with the report.

Independence — Code of Professional Conduct

Three-year bar: no role in assessing an organization you helped prepare for any CMMC assessment in the past three years. It binds the C3PAO as an organization. Disclose in writing; the C3PAO decides whether a conflict can be mitigated. No outcome-contingent fees. Never advise; never predict the outcome.
