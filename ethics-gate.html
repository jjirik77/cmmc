Engagement & Ethics Gate — May This Assessment Proceed?

Every conflict in the Meridian engagement packet, dispositioned individually and cited to the Code of Professional Conduct — then the CAP Phase 1 readiness call.

6 conflicts + 2 gates8 CoPC scenariosCAP Phase 1Prints as D3

The engagement

C3PAOTidewater Assurance Group, LLC — Norfolk, Virginia. Accredited, listed in the Marketplace, in good standing.
OSCMeridian Precision Defense Systems, Inc. — Dayton, Ohio. CAGE 7RK42.
RequestMarcus Ellison, President and CEO, contacted Tidewater directly requesting a Level 2 certification assessment.
Why nowThe Army AMCOM prime contract comes up for recompete and the solicitation requires a current assessment of record.
TimingFieldwork within 120 days. Ellison has asked twice whether it can be sooner.
Prior engagementTidewater conducted a non-certification gap assessment ten months ago.
ContractDraft engagement letter circulated, not yet executed.

What the organization has produced

More than most. A System Security Plan v2.1 from October 2023, marked “needs update” in the company’s own register. Ten written policies, four current and four stale. An accurate network diagram. A CISO who owns all of it. Cloud procured properly — Microsoft 365 GCC High and AWS GovCloud, both FedRAMP High.

And: no asset inventory of any kind. A POA&M held in the CISO’s personal OneDrive outside the authorised CUI boundary, not AO-reviewed in 18 months. An Incident Response Plan dated 2022 that names a departed employee and has never been exercised. Six VPN accounts belonging to separated employees. A managed service provider holding standing domain administrator rights under a month-to-month agreement with no DFARS flow-down and no right to audit. Two security events handled informally, neither assessed against DFARS 252.204-7012, neither reported.

Two questions, in order

First: may this engagement proceed at all? Work every conflict individually. Name the principle and the specific practice, decide the disposition, and say who decides. Some conflicts are in the team biographies. At least one is not.

Then: on the stated assumption that a conflict-free team and organization have been substituted, build the plan. A plan that quietly ignores a disqualifying conflict in order to have something to plan is the exact failure this exercise exists to prevent.

A distinction worth holding as you work

Some defects in this packet are curable — a term can be renegotiated before execution, an individual can be removed from a team. Others are not. Sorting the two is most of the judgement here, and the gate question at the end turns on it.

1

Marcus Reyes — CCA

Employed by Meridian as a systems administrator 2019–2022, where he built the Active Directory group structure and the file server permission model still in use on SYS-003, including the CUI tree. Left on good terms; no services since. Participated in Tidewater’s gap assessment ten months ago.

Model answer

Two separate problems, and neither is cured by disclosure.

First, he would be assessing his own work. The permission model on the CUI tree is his, and it is squarely within scope. That is an impartiality failure under CoPC §2.2 with no mitigation short of removal.

Second, he participated in the gap assessment that produced Appendix C, a Recommended Remediation Sequence. That is advisory activity to prepare the organization, and it engages the three-year bar at 32 CFR §170.8(b)(17)(ii)(G) / CoPC §2.2(d) — which covers any type of CMMC assessment.

Who decides: the C3PAO. Not Reyes, and not the OSC.

2

Priya Raghunathan — CCP

Two years alongside Meridian’s IT Administrator, Trevor Nakamura, on the board of their local professional association chapter; they see each other monthly and occasionally dine with the wider board. No financial or family relationship. Participated in the gap assessment ten months ago.

Model answer

The chapter-board relationship alone would be a disclose-and-mitigate question: an ongoing personal relationship with a key interviewee could reasonably appear to compromise impartiality, and mitigation by reassignment is available. Overcorrecting — treating every professional acquaintance as disqualifying — is its own error.

But she also participated in the gap assessment, which engages the three-year bar independently. Removal follows from the second fact, not the first.

Watch the trap: teams that spot the chapter board and stop have found the softer of the two conflicts and missed the one that actually decides it.

3

Thomas Blalock — CCA

No relationship with Meridian. Holds approximately $30,000 of common stock in Cardinal Industrial Group, a publicly traded company. Cardinal is the parent of ClearPath IT Solutions, the managed service provider that holds standing domain administrator rights over Meridian’s environment. Disclosed the holding to Tidewater when he joined.

Model answer

A general disclosure on joining is not disclosure of a conflict in this engagement. The holding becomes a live conflict the moment ClearPath appears in an assessment scope — and ClearPath is an ESP that will be evaluated inside this assessment, with findings of its own that bear directly on its commercial standing.

The obligation is to disclose in writing as soon as the conflict is known or reasonably should be known. Disposition is the C3PAO’s: mitigation by removing him from the ESP portion is arguable, and removal from the team is cleaner. What is not available is Blalock deciding it is immaterial because the interest is in the parent rather than the subsidiary.

4

Tidewater Assurance Group — the firm

Conducted a non-certification gap assessment for Meridian ten months ago. Three days on site at Dayton. The 26-page deliverable included Appendix C — Recommended Remediation Sequence, a prioritized plan with suggested tooling for five of the eight gaps and estimated timelines and costs. Meridian’s POA&M was built directly from Section 4 of that report.

Model answer

This is the conflict the packet does not label, and it is the one that decides the engagement.

The three-year bar binds the C3PAO as an organization, not only individual assessors. Tidewater consulted to prepare Meridian for a CMMC assessment within three years: a recommended remediation sequence with suggested tooling and cost estimates is advisory activity by any reading, and the company’s own POA&M is built from it.

Substituting personnel does not cure a firm-level bar. Where a conflict cannot be sufficiently mitigated, the C3PAO does not proceed. Tidewater must decline.

A D3 that quietly ignores this in order to have something to plan has failed the exercise regardless of how good the rest of the plan is.

5

The engagement letter

Outcome provision: “In the event a Certificate of CMMC Status is not issued, thirty percent (30%) of the fee shall be credited against a subsequent assessment conducted by Tidewater within twelve months.”
Support provision: “Tidewater will make its assessors available to answer implementation questions arising from the assessment for a period of 60 days following the out-brief.”

Model answer

Both terms are prohibited, for different reasons.

The outcome provision creates a financial interest contingent on the certificate issuing: the firm keeps the full fee only if it does. The contract may not include guarantees, promises of outcome, or any incentive or bonus contingent on a certificate being issued. Reading it as ordinary commercial risk-sharing is exactly how such a term survives review.

The support provision is post-assessment consulting sold inside an assessment engagement. “Answering implementation questions” is advising on remediation, and it also creates an interest in a continuing relationship with the assessed party.

Note the distinction that matters for the gate below: these terms are curable. The letter has not been executed, and a firm that strikes both provisions has fixed this problem. Mark it as requiring the engagement to be declined as currently drafted — and keep it separate in your mind from the firm-level bar, which no amount of redrafting will fix.

6

Ellison’s request

“Can you take a pass at the asset inventory and tidy up the SSP first, so we are not wasting your people’s time?” He has also asked twice whether fieldwork can be compressed.

Model answer

The request is reasonable-sounding, made in good faith, by a company that has genuinely tried and is short of one artifact. The answer is still no, and being able to say why without being prompted and without lecturing him is what Lesson 3 has been building toward.

Building the asset inventory and revising the SSP is preparing the organization for the assessment. Doing it would place Tidewater squarely inside the three-year bar for any future certification assessment of Meridian, and it would mean the firm later assesses documents it authored.

Note the second half too: an adverse readiness determination is a legitimate Phase 1 outcome, and under no circumstances does the C3PAO, the team, or anyone affiliated with it then help the OSC get ready. The consulting bar arrives at the moment it is hardest to honour.

On the schedule pressure: compressing fieldwork is a scoping and sufficiency question, not a courtesy. If the compressed schedule would not support accurate determinations, it is refused on that ground.

7

The gate: may this engagement proceed?

Having worked every conflict, answer the question Phase 1 exists to answer — and be precise about which defect is disqualifying, because more than one is prohibited and only one cannot be fixed.

Model answer

No. Tidewater must decline — on the firm-level bar.

Tidewater consulted to prepare Meridian for a CMMC assessment within three years, through a gap assessment whose Appendix C was a recommended remediation sequence with suggested tooling, and from which the company built its POA&M. The bar at 32 CFR §170.8(b)(17)(ii)(G) binds the organization, not only individual assessors, so substituting personnel cannot cure it.

Why not the engagement letter? Both terms are prohibited and both must be struck — but they are curable. A contract not yet executed can be renegotiated, and a firm that removes the outcome provision and the support provision has fixed that problem. The three-year bar cannot be renegotiated, cannot be cured by substituting personnel, and does not expire until it expires. Distinguishing a disqualifying defect from a correctable one is the judgement this item is testing, and a team that answers “the contract terms” has identified a real problem and the wrong one.

Three individual conflicts would also require dispositions. None of that is reached, because the engagement fails at the firm level first.

What would have to change: a different, unconflicted C3PAO — which is exactly what happens in the case. Eight months later Meridian engages Ridgeline Compliance Group, and that is the assessment you run on Day 5.

8

Phase 1: is Meridian ready to be assessed?

Assume the conflict problem solved. Phase 1 asks one question — not whether the company will pass, but whether the evaluative work of Phase 2 can sensibly begin. This is a closer call than it looks.

Model answer

Not ready — and the reason has to be specific. An adverse readiness determination is a legitimate Phase 1 outcome and the paperwork is still completed.

Meridian is a harder Phase 1 than a company with nothing. The artifacts the CAP requires Phase 1 to review mostly exist: an SSP can be reviewed for completeness, accuracy and consistency; policies can be read; a network diagram can be examined. On a checklist reading this company is ready.

It fails on one thing. Scope cannot be validated under 32 CFR §170.19(c), because no asset inventory exists. The Lead CCA is required to validate scope, scope disagreements must be resolved before Phase 2 may begin, and there is no enumeration of assets against which to do either.

Why not “the SSP is out of date”? It is out of date, and that is a Phase 2 problem. Phase 1’s SSP review is cursory — completeness, accuracy and consistency — not currency of every control description. Reaching the right conclusion by the wrong route will not survive a quality review.

Risks stated correctly, without fixes:

  • No asset inventory exists; the assessment scope cannot be validated under §170.19(c).
  • ClearPath IT Solutions holds standing domain administrator rights over CUI-bearing systems, has not been informed of the assessment, and its agreement contains no obligation to support one.
  • The POA&M is held outside the authorised CUI boundary, contradicting the SSP’s own boundary statement.
  • Three CUI-handling suppliers have no adequate flow-down; one has no written agreement at all.
  • Two security events were closed without documented analysis against DFARS 252.204-7012.

What none of these say: what Meridian should do about any of it. Including in response to Ellison’s request.

The eight CoPC scenarios

Worked in session and adjudicated against the text. For each: name the principle and the specific practice, decide the disposition, and say who decides. One of the eight is not addressed directly by the Code at all — finding that out is part of the exercise.

Scenario 1 — The former employer

You are a CCA. Four years ago you spent three years as a systems administrator at the company your C3PAO has just been engaged to assess. You built the file-share permission structure, and it is still in place. You left on good terms and have never provided CMMC consulting to anyone.

Show the adjudication

Adjudication

You would be assessing your own work. The three-year bar is not engaged — you never consulted for a CMMC assessment — but impartiality under CoPC §2.2 plainly is, and the structure is still in scope. Disclose; the available mitigation is removal from the team, and the C3PAO decides.

Scenario 2 — The spouse

Your spouse is a program manager at the OSC. They work on client delivery, have no IT or security responsibilities, and no involvement in the CMMC effort. You have not mentioned the relationship to anyone at your C3PAO, on the basis that it has nothing to do with the systems being assessed.

Show the adjudication

Adjudication

The failure here is the non-disclosure, not the relationship. A family relationship with an employee of the OSC is a textbook impartiality threat and the CoPC’s own worked examples mitigate it by removing the assessor from the team. Deciding for yourself that it is immaterial is precisely what you may not do.

Scenario 3 — The templates

Your C3PAO has a sister company operating as an RPO. Eighteen months ago that RPO sold the OSC a package of CMMC policy templates and a gap-analysis workbook. No person advised the OSC directly; it bought the materials and implemented them itself. The OSC now wants your C3PAO to conduct its Level 2 certification assessment.

Show the adjudication

Adjudication

Supplying templates, workbooks, or tools that guide implementation is advisory activity. The bar reaches organizations, and a sister company inside the same group does not create distance. Whether a human gave advice is not the test. Expect this to be argued; the argument does not survive the text.

Scenario 4 — The drafted finding

To save time on a long evidence review, a team member pastes several paragraphs of the OSC’s system security plan and a screenshot of its access-control configuration into a public web-based AI assistant, and asks it to draft finding language. They read the output, edit it, and enter it as their determination.

Show the adjudication

Adjudication

Two breaches, not one. Confidentiality — engagement-confidential material describing how to attack the OSC left the boundary. Proper Use of Technology and AI — a tool produced reasoning that is the credentialed assessor’s to produce. Information Integrity is arguably a third. Editing the output cures neither of the first two. Report to the C3PAO.

Scenario 5 — The portfolio

On the last day of on-site work the OSC’s chief executive presents each team member with a leather portfolio embossed with the company logo, worth roughly $150. He is warm and slightly awkward about it and says explicitly that it carries no expectation, whatever the outcome. The determination has not been issued.

Show the adjudication

Adjudication

Timing and value both matter, and the determination is still open. Accepting a gift of this value from the assessed party before determinations are issued creates an appearance problem that no amount of stated disinterest removes. Decline graciously, and disclose that it was offered.

Scenario 6 — The marginal control

One security requirement is genuinely borderline — the evidence is thin and a reasonable assessor could go either way. The OSC’s point of contact mentions in passing that a $4.7M award depends on the certificate and source selection closes in three weeks. Separately, your project manager mentions the engagement is over budget and asks whether the remaining items can be closed out quickly.

Show the adjudication

Adjudication

Neither piece of information bears on the evidence. Both are pressure — one commercial to the OSC, one commercial to your own firm, and the second is the more dangerous because it comes from inside. Decide the requirement on the objectives, document the thinness of the evidence, and disclose the budget pressure if it is affecting the assessment. Note that the CoPC’s impartiality principle names commercial pressure explicitly.

Scenario 7 — The referral

At the out-brief the OSC asks who could help remediate the gaps you identified. A team member names a specific remediation firm, adding that they are excellent and he knows the principal well. Two months later that firm pays him a referral fee. He does not mention it to the C3PAO.

Show the adjudication

Adjudication

Three failures compounding. Naming a remediation firm at an out-brief is advice. Naming one he has a relationship with is a conflict. Accepting an undisclosed fee arising from an assessment he conducted is a Professionalism and Impartiality breach that would end a career. The correct answer to the question asked is that the assessment team cannot make referrals.

Scenario 8 — The chapter board

You and the OSC’s IT Administrator have both served on the board of your local professional association chapter for two years. You see each other at monthly meetings and occasionally have dinner with the wider board afterwards. There is no financial relationship, no family connection, and you have never advised the company.

Show the adjudication

Adjudication

This is the scenario the CoPC does not address directly, and finding that out is part of the exercise. Say so plainly and reason from the principles: the test in §2.2 is whether something could compromise the determination or reasonably appear to. An ongoing social relationship with a key interviewee reaches that. Disclose; the C3PAO decides whether reassignment suffices. An honest “the document does not cover this, so here is what governs” is stronger than a citation that does not say what you claim it says.
