Day 2 · Morning · CCP Domain 3 (15%)

FCI, CUI, and the Regulatory Stack

The determination everything downstream depends on. Get this wrong and the level is wrong, the scope is wrong, the evidence plan is wrong, and every finding inherits the error. This is also the cheapest session in which to make a mistake, which is why it comes early.

Day 2 — AM ~3 hours CCP 15% Tool: CUI Determination

Regulatory status as of 16 November 2026: Phase 2 is suspended; check again before you teach.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to…

  • Distinguish Federal Contract Information from Controlled Unclassified Information by definition and by consequence — not by how sensitive the information feels. Bloom: Analyze
  • Use the NARA CUI Registry to identify the category, marking, Basic/Specified status, and authorizing law for a given information type. Bloom: Apply
  • Audit a contractor's own data classification and say precisely where and why it is wrong. Bloom: Analyze
  • Explain why a contractor identifies and protects CUI but does not designate it, and say who does. Bloom: Understand
  • Determine the DFARS 252.204-7012 obligations attaching to a contract, including the 72-hour clock, media preservation, cloud requirements, and flowdown. Bloom: Apply
  • State which systems fall inside the CUI boundary and defend the line. Bloom: Evaluate

1

Two definitions, and what follows from each

Start with the text rather than the intuition. Both definitions are short, and both turn on origin rather than sensitivity.

Federal Contract Information — FAR 52.204-21(a)

Information, not intended for public release, that is provided by or generated for the Government under a contract to develop or deliver a product or service to the Government. It excludes information the Government provides to the public — anything on a public website — and simple transactional information such as that necessary to process payments.

Controlled Unclassified Information — 32 CFR 2002.4

Information the Government creates or possesses, or that an entity creates or possesses for or on behalf of the Government, that a law, regulation, or government-wide policy requires or permits an agency to handle with safeguarding or dissemination controls. It excludes classified information, and information a company develops purely at its own expense for its own purposes.

Meridian holds CUI under all three contracts, and FCI besides. The Army prime carries the CMMC clause expressly; the Raytheon subcontract and the NAVAIR SBIR carry DFARS 252.204-7012 without it. All of it lives on the same file server, in the same tenant, handled by the same people. The obligations stack; they do not partition themselves because the contracts are separate, and a company cannot run three security postures across one network.

2

Who decides

This is the part practitioners get wrong most often, and the error is not subtle once you see it.

Under EO 13556 and 32 CFR Part 2002, information becomes CUI because a law, regulation, or government-wide policy requires it to be controlled, and an authorized holder in the designating agency applies that basis. A contractor is not a designating agency. A contractor cannot make information CUI by marking it, and cannot make it not-CUI by declining to.

A contractor mayA contractor may not
Identify information already designated as CUI by the GovernmentDesignate information as CUI on its own authority
Apply required markings when creating documents from CUI it holdsDecide that CUI it received is “not really” CUI
Ask the contracting officer for a determination when status is unclearMark commercial or internal information CUI “to be safe”
Protect the information at the level the contract requiresDecontrol CUI — only the designating agency can
Flow the requirement down to subcontractors who will handle itRely on the absence of markings as proof that nothing is CUI
Unmarked does not mean unprotected

You will find CUI in the field carrying no marking at all — because a government office failed to mark it, because it was extracted from a marked document, or because it was generated during performance and nobody thought about it. The obligation attaches to the information, not to the sticker. “It was not marked” is an explanation for how a company got into trouble. It has never been a defence.

3

The Registry, used properly

The NARA CUI Registry is the authoritative list of CUI categories, each tied to the authority that permits the control. Using it well is a mechanical skill and you should be fast at it by the end of the session.

For any information type, produce four things:

  1. The exact category name as the Registry states it — not a paraphrase, and not a name the company made up.
  2. The category marking, and the banner marking that results.
  3. Whether the category is CUI Basic or CUI Specified.
  4. The authorizing law, regulation, or government-wide policy the Registry cites.

Exam tip — the SP- prefix

A banner such as CUI//SP-CTI marks a CUI Specified category — one where the authorizing law, regulation, or policy itself sets handling requirements beyond the CUI Basic baseline. Some categories are Specified under one authority and Basic under another. That is why you read the category page rather than memorizing a list.

4

DFARS 252.204-7012, clause by clause

This clause predates CMMC by seven years and stands entirely independently of it. It is the reason Meridian, with no certification and no assessor ever having visited, is already in breach of its Army prime contract. Know it in detail.

ObligationWhat the clause requiresWhere
Adequate securityImplement NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 on covered contractor information systems; deviations require a written determination by the DoD CIO.(b)(2)
Rapid reportingReport a cyber incident to DoD within 72 hours of discovery, via DIBNet, using a DoD-approved medium assurance certificate.(c)(1)
Malicious softwareSubmit malicious software discovered and isolated in connection with a reported incident to the DoD Cyber Crime Center. Do not send it with the incident report.(d)
Media preservationPreserve and protect images of known affected systems and relevant monitoring data for at least 90 days from the incident report.(e)
DoD accessProvide DoD access to additional information or equipment necessary for forensic analysis on request.(f)
Cloud servicesRequire an external cloud service provider storing, processing, or transmitting CDI to meet the FedRAMP Moderate baseline or equivalent, and to comply with the reporting, preservation, and access requirements above.(b)(2)(ii)(D)
FlowdownInclude the clause, without alteration except to identify the parties, in subcontracts for operationally critical support or where performance will involve CDI.(m)
Three details worth memorizing exactly

72 hours, from discovery of the incident — not from confirmation, not from the end of the investigation.
90 days of media and monitoring-data preservation, running from submission of the incident report.
A medium assurance certificate is required to file the report at all. Meridian has never obtained one, which means that on the day it needs to report it will discover this at hour 70 of a 72-hour clock.

The cloud question at Meridian, precisely

Meridian keeps CUI in Microsoft 365 GCC High and runs SBIR analysis in AWS GovCloud. Both carry FedRAMP High authorisations — comfortably above the Moderate baseline the clause names. So is (b)(2)(ii)(D) satisfied?

The authorisation is satisfied. The obligation may not be. Two things remain to check.

First, the clause requires the provider to comply with the cyber incident reporting, media preservation, and DoD access requirements as well. A FedRAMP authorisation is a statement about the platform's security posture; it is not by itself a commitment to those contractual obligations, and the assessor asks what in the agreement establishes them.

Second — and this is where Meridian actually fails — an authorised platform that is misconfigured is not a compliant environment. Conditional Access is only partially configured, guest access to SharePoint is not fully restricted, and a shared service account holds Global Admin with no individual owner assigned. The platform is doing its job. The tenant is not.

Note how this differs from the more familiar failure. A contractor keeping CUI in a commercial tenant fails at the authorisation. Meridian passes the authorisation and fails at the configuration — which is harder to see, easier to argue about, and far more common in companies that have spent money on the right products.

5

Auditing a contractor's own inventory

Meridian maintains a data inventory, and like most contractors it produced that inventory without ever opening the Registry. Your job is not to accept it. It is to audit it.

Six entries. Some of the company's calls are right. Among the wrong ones there is a category name that does not exist, an item the company has treated as its own commercial property when the contract says otherwise, an internal contradiction between two of the company's own records, and one item classified too low — the direction that costs money.

#InformationMeridian says

6Cost and pricing data under the CPFF Raytheon subcontract — indirect cost pools, negotiated labour rates, and the build-ups behind themFCI
The contradiction to resolve, not average

Meridian's data classification page states plainly that payroll, ESOP participant records, and commercial supplier invoices are sensitive business data that do not meet the CUI definition. Its third-party register, three screens away, records Whitmore & Associates — the external CPA with read-only QuickBooks access — as handling “employee PII / CUI-Privacy,” while the same register elsewhere states the firm is “not in SSP scope.”

Both cannot be right, and neither is authority — the Registry and the definition are. Saying what distinguishes item 5 from item 3, since both concern employees, is the heart of the audit. The distinction is not sensitivity. It is who the information was created for. SF-86 and DISS material is collected on the Government's behalf under a personnel security process; a W-4 and an ESOP statement are not.

The item the company has quietly reclassified as its own

Item 4 is the interesting one. Meridian calls the SBIR research “proprietary R&D — company confidential,” which is how a great many contractors think about work they performed. But the NAVAIR contract identifies CUI//SP-EXPT, and the CUI definition expressly reaches information an entity creates or possesses for or on behalf of the Government.

SBIR data rights are real and they are a different question entirely: a contractor may hold data rights in technical data and that information may still be CUI. Rights govern what the Government may do with it. CUI status governs how it must be protected. Conflating the two is the specific error here, and it is extremely common in research-performing contractors.

An assessor who says “this is CUI//SP-EXPT under the contract, and your data rights position is unaffected by that” has made the determination and defused the argument in one sentence.

Where Meridian is right

Item 2 is correct and should be marked so. Note what makes it correct: Meridian generated the FEA reports and drawings itself, and they are still CUI, because covered defense information reaches information the contractor develops in performance. Contractors routinely assume that only what the Government hands them counts.

6

Drawing the boundary

The last question is the hardest half: which systems are inside the CUI boundary, and which are outside.

Dayton runs three subnets — CORP-LAN (10.10.1.0/24), ENG-LAN (10.10.2.0/24) and MGMT-LAN (10.10.3.0/24) — and all three route freely through a core Cisco Catalyst 2960-X. There is no VLAN-enforced segmentation between them, so a compromise on CORP-LAN has direct SMB and NFS access to the CUI file stores on ENG-LAN. MGMT-LAN is separated by policy only, not at the switch. Huntsville (192.168.5.0/24) sits behind a Cisco RV345 on an always-on IPsec tunnel back to the Dayton domain controllers.

Multifactor authentication is enforced at the Microsoft 365 GCC High layer. It is not enforced at the network access layer: the FortiGate SSL VPN authenticates against Active Directory with username and password alone, roughly 22 users hold VPN access, and six of those accounts belong to people who no longer work here.

A boundary is named systems

“The network” is not a boundary. Name systems, say what puts each inside or outside, and say what evidence would move one across the line. A strong determination also notices the shape of the answer here: the boundary is wide by default rather than by design, because there is no segmentation and no policy — and that fact is itself the most consequential finding available.

7

The Meridian case

Meridian · Day 2 Morning · Deliverable D2 · Fictional case

Sort the data inventory: FCI, CUI, or neither

Six information types, six company classifications, and your determination on each. The CUI Determination tool gives you the Registry categories, the definitions, and a workspace, then checks your calls one at a time with the reasoning — not just the verdict.

For each item you determine is CUI: the exact Registry category name, the marking, Basic or Specified, and the authority the Registry cites. For each item you determine is FCI or neither: the element of the definition it fails, and which definition you applied.

Then say what kind of error the company made where it is wrong — a category that does not exist, a category that exists but does not fit, an internal contradiction, or an item classified too low.

A warning about your source material. The inventory reproduces Meridian's own classifications. Those are assertions by the party being assessed, which is precisely the kind of claim you are being trained not to accept. If a determination in your memo can be traced only to something the contractor said about itself, it is not yet a determination.

For faculty

Faculty teaching segment

  • Signature assignment: Students classify six information types for a fictional company (the D2 deliverable): for each CUI item, the exact NARA Registry category, marking, Basic or Specified, and authority; for each FCI or "neither" item, the element of the definition it fails.
  • Rubric criteria:
    • Registry categories are named exactly as the Registry names them
    • FCI and "neither" calls cite the definition element that decides them
    • Each company error is named by type (a category that does not exist, one that does not fit, a contradiction, or an under-classification)
    • Items the Registry cannot settle are routed to the contract and the contracting officer, not guessed
  • Lab idea: Project the NARA CUI Registry and have students look items up live in the CUI Determination tool rather than reading from a key.
  • Course fit: Data governance, privacy, information security management, compliance.
🎓 Teaching noteOne item is worth half the session: students want to keep a category because it is nearly right. Let them fail to find a fitting one and land on the contracting officer. That discomfort is correct.

Knowledge Check

Test your judgment

Ten questions in CCP style. Read every rationale.

Q1.Who may designate information as CUI?

Question 1 options
Correct: D. Designation authority sits with the Government under EO 13556 and 32 CFR Part 2002, and it must rest on an authority that requires or permits the control. Why the others fall short: A A contractor identifies and protects CUI. It does not designate, and it cannot decontrol. Almost every bad determination in the field is a variation on a contractor believing it holds an authority it does not hold.; B Handling authority is not designation authority. An authorized holder may possess and handle CUI in accordance with 32 CFR Part 2002 without any power to designate it.; C A prime flows down obligations. It does not acquire designation authority by being higher in the supply chain.

Q2.Meridian classifies its SBIR final technical report as "proprietary R&D — company confidential" rather than CUI. What is the assessor's response?

Question 2 options
Correct: C. The NAVAIR contract identifies the category, and 32 CFR 2002.4 expressly reaches information an entity creates or possesses for or on behalf of the Government. The company's data rights position is unaffected by the determination. Why the others fall short: A Conflates two separate questions. Data rights govern what the Government may do with the information; CUI status governs how it must be protected. A contractor can hold data rights in information that is also CUI.; B Marking is evidence, not the test. The obligation attaches to the information, and the contract identifies the category regardless.; D It was not performed at the contractor's own initiative. It was performed under a $1.85M federal contract that names the CUI category.

Q3.Meridian keeps CUI in Microsoft 365 GCC High, which holds a FedRAMP High authorization. Its Conditional Access policies are partially configured, guest access to SharePoint is unrestricted, and a shared service account holds Global Admin. What follows?

Question 3 options
Correct: B. This is the harder and more common failure. Meridian passed the procurement question and failed the implementation one — and the misconfigurations are findings against access control and configuration requirements in their own right. Why the others fall short: A The authorization is satisfied. It is a statement about the platform, and it says nothing about how this tenant is configured.; C No such rule exists. The clause names the FedRAMP Moderate baseline or equivalent, and GCC High comfortably exceeds it.; D Tenant configuration is the customer's responsibility, not the provider's. That allocation is exactly what a Customer Responsibility Matrix exists to make explicit.

Q4.Within what period must a cyber incident be reported under DFARS 252.204-7012, and from when?

Question 4 options
Correct: A. Via DIBNet, and a DoD-approved medium assurance certificate is required to file at all. Why the others fall short: B Neither the period nor the trigger is right.; C The reading contractors act on in practice, and it is wrong. Meridian acted on something close to this and then never finished the investigation either.; D No provision of the clause supports this.

Q5.Meridian's payroll records, production labour hours, and commercial vendor invoices are best classified as:

Question 5 options
Correct: A. Created and held by Meridian as an employer and a business, at its own expense, for its own purposes. Expressly outside the CUI definition at 32 CFR 2002.4 and outside FCI at FAR 52.204-21(a). Why the others fall short: B Sensitive and regulated, but neither is the test. Ask what law requires an agency to control it. Nothing does — this material was never created for or on behalf of the Government.; C Fails the FCI definition too: it was not provided by or generated for the Government under either contract.; D PROCURE concerns government acquisition material. Invoices from Meridian's own commercial raw-material suppliers are not that.

Q6.Employee clearance submissions (SF-86 data) are CUI, but ordinary payroll records are not. What distinguishes them?

Question 6 options
Correct: B. The distinction is who the information was created for. SF-86 material is collected on the Government's behalf; a W-4 is not. Both concern employees; only one is inside the CUI definition. Why the others fall short: A Sensitivity is not the test, and reasoning from it is how contractors end up over-marking everything.; C CUI is unclassified by definition, and no clearance attaches to it.; D Who processes it is irrelevant to whether it is CUI. The external accountant's involvement changes the scoping analysis, not the classification.

Q7.Meridian classifies its contract pricing and cost data as FCI. What is the strongest assessor response?

Question 7 options
Correct: C. PROCURE covers cost or pricing data, contract information, indirect costs, and direct labour rates on its face, and it is Specified. This is the item classified too low — the direction that costs money, because the contracts folder has been handled at the FCI standard. Why the others fall short: A The transactional exclusion in FAR 52.204-21(a) covers information necessary to process payments, not cost proposals and negotiated indirect rates.; B Source Selection covers pre-award proposal evaluation material held by the Government. Close, and not this.; D Contractor-generated information is squarely inside both the CUI definition and the covered defense information definition.

Q8.What does a banner marking of CUI//SP-CTI indicate?

Question 8 options
Correct: D. The SP- prefix denotes Specified. Note that some categories are Specified under one authority and Basic under another, which is why you read the category page rather than memorizing a list. Why the others fall short: A CUI is unclassified. Nothing about a CUI marking implies classification.; B No clearance or special permission regime attaches to CUI as such; lawful government purpose and any limited dissemination controls govern access.; C Specified is a government-wide construct, not a DoD one.

Q9.An assessor finds CUI in a contractor's SharePoint carrying no CUI markings at all. What follows?

Question 9 options
Correct: C. Government offices fail to mark; material gets extracted from marked documents; information is generated during performance and nobody thinks about it. 252.204-7012 also reaches information "otherwise identified in the contract." Why the others fall short: A The obligation attaches to the information, not to the marking. This is the defence contractors reach for and it has never worked.; B A contractor applies required markings when it creates documents from CUI it holds. It does not designate, and marking something does not settle its status.; D Only the designating agency can decontrol CUI, and an assessor asking for anything to be changed has stopped assessing.

Q10.ClearPath maintains Meridian's PLM, which holds CUI, with standing remote VPN access under a master services agreement containing no security terms. Under DFARS 252.204-7012, what is missing?

Question 10 options
Correct: A. Performance plainly involves CDI — the PLM holds it and ClearPath has standing access. The clause must be included without alteration except to identify the parties. There is also a separate maintenance requirement at MA.L2-3.7.5 for nonlocal maintenance sessions. Why the others fall short: B Paragraph (m) turns on whether performance will involve covered defense information, not on what the parties call the relationship.; C FedRAMP applies to cloud service providers. ClearPath is a non-cloud external service provider, which is a different rule with different consequences — you will separate the two on Day 3.; D Monitoring would address a different requirement. It does not discharge the flowdown obligation, and in any event Meridian does not monitor them.
