Clause Trace — From Executive Order to Contract

Build the chain that binds Meridian to solicitation W58RGZ-26-R-0301, then answer the three questions the Regulatory Trace Memo asks. Primary sources only, cited to part, section, or clause.

3 questionsPrimary sources onlyPrints as D1

The solicitation

W58RGZ-26-R-0301 — Army AMCOM, Clothing & Textiles. Flame-Resistant Combat Uniform Coat, Improved Pattern. IDIQ, firm fixed price, $12.4M over five years, total small business set-aside. Meridian Precision Defense Systems is the incumbent; the expiring contract is $11.4M of its $17.9M annual DoD revenue.

Section C — Statement of Work (extract)

  • C.3.2 On award the Government furnishes the complete Technical Data Package: engineering drawings, pattern grading specifications, seam and stitch construction standards, flame-resistance test protocols, approved materials list. Portions bear Distribution Statement D and are subject to the export control provisions cited in Section I.
  • C.3.3 The contractor maintains and transmits monthly production status reports, first-article test results, lot traceability records, and deviation requests, referencing drawing numbers and revision levels from the TDP.
  • C.4.1 Any subcontractor that will receive, store, process, or transmit any portion of the TDP shall be identified to the Contracting Officer, and the safeguarding and certification requirements of Section I shall be included in that subcontract.

Section I — Contract clauses (partial)

FAR 52.204-21 · DFARS 252.204-7008 · DFARS 252.204-7012 · DFARS 252.204-7019 · DFARS 252.204-7020 · DFARS 252.204-7021 — Required CMMC Status: ______ (see Section L.5) · DFARS 252.225-7048 · DFARS 252.247-7023

Section L.5 — Cybersecurity representations

Offerors shall, at proposal submission, have a current assessment of record posted in SPRS covering all information systems that will process, store, or transmit information furnished under C.3.2. The Contracting Officer will verify status in SPRS prior to award. The CMMC status required by DFARS 252.204-7021 shall be that which corresponds to the categories of information identified in Section C, determined in accordance with 32 CFR Part 170.

Note what the solicitation does not do

It never states a level in plain language. The fill-in is blank and Section L points you back to Section C. The level is a function of the information, and the information is described in the Statement of Work — so you read Section C before Section I.

1

What authority makes CMMC applicable to this solicitation?

Trace it. Name the instruments in order and say what each contributes. Primary sources only — cite to part, section, or clause. A law firm alert may have oriented you; it does not appear in your answer.

Model answer

The operative chain for this solicitation:

  • 32 CFR Part 170 establishes the CMMC Program and, at §170.14, the level structure. This is the instrument Section L.5 expressly points to.
  • The 48 CFR acquisition rule (eff. 10 Nov 2025) amended the DFARS so the Contracting Officer may insert the CMMC clause — which is why 252.204-7021 appears in Section I at all.
  • DFARS 252.204-7021 is the clause that makes a CMMC status a condition of award and of continued performance.
  • Underneath it, and independent of it: DFARS 252.204-7012 imposes adequate security via NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 and the 72-hour report; DFARS 252.204-7019/-7020 require the SPRS assessment of record that Section L.5 says will be verified; and FAR 52.204-21 applies to FCI.
  • Further back: NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 specifies the requirements but binds no one on its own; 32 CFR Part 2002 implements the CUI Program; EO 13556 created it.

Assessor-Ready adds: where the chain differs for a subcontractor. C.4.1 and DFARS 252.204-7012(m) put the obligation on any subcontractor receiving the TDP — the obligation follows the information, not the tier.

2

What CMMC level, and what evidence must exist at award?

Reason from Section C, not from the clause list.

Model answer

Level 2. C.3.2 describes engineering drawings, pattern grading specifications, construction standards, and test protocols, with portions bearing Distribution Statement D and subject to export control. That is technical information with military application subject to controls on access, use, reproduction, modification, release, or dissemination — Controlled Technical Information, a CUI category in the NARA Registry's Defense index. CUI drives Level 2 under 32 CFR 170.14.

C.3.3 matters independently: production reports, first-article results, and lot traceability records referencing TDP drawing numbers are information the contractor generates during performance, and DFARS 252.204-7012's definition of covered defense information captures exactly that.

Evidence at award: Section L.5 requires a current assessment of record posted in SPRS covering all systems that will process, store, or transmit the C.3.2 information, verified by the Contracting Officer before award. An offeror without one is ineligible. Meridian has never calculated or submitted a score.

3

Which obligations would survive if the CMMC clause were struck tomorrow?

Ellison will ask what the company is actually exposed to, and whether any of this can wait. Answer in terms he can act on.

Model answer

What CMMC adds is the verification regime: a stated level and status as a condition of award under DFARS 252.204-7021, assessed under 32 CFR Part 170.

What binds the company independently of it: DFARS 252.204-7012 — adequate security via all 110 NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 requirements, the 72-hour DIBNet report, malicious-software submission, 90-day media preservation, DoD access on request, the cloud-provider conditions, and flowdown at (m). DFARS 252.204-7019 and -7020 and the SPRS assessment of record. FAR 52.204-21 wherever FCI is handled. None of that arrives with CMMC and none of it leaves with it.

What that means for Meridian today: all three contracts carry 252.204-7012. The company has an unenforced MFA gap on the VPN, six active accounts belonging to separated employees, no reviewed audit logs, and two security events that were never assessed against the clause's reporting threshold and never reported. That is not a future exposure contingent on an assessment — it is present non-conformity with a clause the company signed.

The distinction to hold
CMMC changes how conformity is demonstrated. It does not change what must be true. An assessor who cannot state that cleanly will be talked into treating programme changes as relief from the underlying obligation.
