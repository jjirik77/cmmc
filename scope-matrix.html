Scope & Asset Applicability Matrix

Every asset in the Meridian environment, categorized against the defining test and justified — then the boundary, the provider analysis, flowdown, and the open items.

14 assets5 providersBoundary + flowdownPrints as D4

Before you categorize anything

Each category answers one question: what does this asset do with CUI? Fill in what an asset can reach before you decide what it is.

CategoryThe defining testHow it is assessed
CUI AssetProcesses, stores, or transmits CUIAll Level 2 requirements
Security Protection AssetProvides security functions or capabilities to the scope, whether or not it touches CUIRequirements relevant to the function provided
Contractor Risk ManagedCan, but is not intended to, handle CUI because of security policy, procedures, and practices in placeSSP review primarily
Specialized AssetCan handle CUI but is unable to be fully secured. Closed list: IoT, IIoT, OT, GFE, Restricted Information Systems, Test EquipmentSSP review only
Out-of-ScopeCannot handle CUI and provides no security protection for CUI AssetsNot assessed

The condition that decides this matrix

Two categories are conditional. Contractor Risk Managed depends on security policy, procedures, and practices being in place. Specialized depends on documentation in the inventory, the SSP, and the network diagram, plus demonstrable management through risk-based security policies.

Meridian has an SSP (version 2.1, October 2023, out of date), written security policies, and a network diagram, but no real asset inventory. Both conditional categories require the asset to be documented in the inventory, so both are closed to it, and assets fall back to what they are capable of doing. That single fact decides most of what follows — and granting relief the organization has not earned is the most common scoping error in the field.

The asymmetry to remember: scoping an asset in that did not need to be in costs the organization time. Scoping an asset out that should have been in produces an assessment result that is wrong. When you are genuinely uncertain, the direction to err is not the comfortable one.

23

The assessment boundary

State it in prose. Name what is inside, name what is outside, and say what places each there. If nothing is outside, say that and say why — it is a legitimate answer and it needs the same defence as any other.

Model answer

Everything at Dayton and Huntsville is inside the boundary except Whitmore & Associates and FastenerTech. Not because the assessor is being difficult, but because of two specific absences.

First, no enforced segmentation. CORP-LAN, ENG-LAN and MGMT-LAN route freely through the core switch, so every machine on CORP-LAN can reach the CUI stores on ENG-LAN. Meridian has three subnets and one security domain.

Second, no asset inventory. Both conditional categories require the asset to be documented in an inventory that does not exist. That single gap closes Contractor Risk Managed and Specialized to every asset in the environment, however well an individual asset might otherwise fit.

Note what makes this different from a company with nothing. Meridian has an SSP, ten policies, and a network diagram. It still cannot claim the conditional treatments, because the specific artifact those treatments depend on is the one it does not have. Saying that precisely — rather than “they have no documentation” — is the difference between a Competent and an Assessor-Ready boundary statement.

A hostile reader who wants the boundary smaller should not be able to move any line in it.

24

Flowdown

Which of Meridian’s suppliers, subcontractors, or providers must receive DFARS 252.204-7012, and on what basis? Include anyone downstream who will handle covered defense information.

Model answer

DFARS 252.204-7012(m) requires the clause in subcontracts for operationally critical support or where performance will involve covered defense information. Meridian has four parties in that category and a written flow-down for none of them.

PartyBasisCurrent state
ClearPath IT SolutionsStanding domain admin over systems holding CUI; also handles Security Protection DataMonth-to-month agreement with no DFARS flow-down, no cybersecurity requirements, no right to audit
Acuitas MachiningReceives CUI drawings via a SharePoint link to perform machining workNo formal agreement at all
AeroComp CompositesReceives 2D drawings by encrypted emailReferenced in the purchase order, never verified — ask what evidence shows the clause reached and was accepted
Secure Shred ALTakes physical custody of hard-copy CUI at HuntsvilleNo NDA, no CUI handling agreement

FastenerTech receives no CUI and requires no flow-down — say what you checked before concluding it.

The observation that marks a strong answer: the obligation follows the information, not the contract value or the tier. Secure Shred is a shredding vendor and Acuitas is an overflow machine shop; neither looks like a defense subcontractor, and both handle CUI. Meridian’s Contract Manager tracks DFARS clauses on the three prime and subcontract vehicles and has not applied the same discipline downward.

25

Open items

What you could not determine from the available material, why it matters to the scope, and what evidence would settle each. There are at least two.

Model answer

Two that should appear in every competent matrix:

  • What the Xerox AltaLink retains on internal storage, and for how long. An enterprise MFP that spools and retains CUI print and scan jobs is an information system holding CUI, and the case does not say whether this one does. Settled by examining the device’s storage configuration directly — an inspection, not an interview.
  • Whether an asset inventory exists in any form. It is the condition on both conditional categories. If nothing exists, Contractor Risk Managed and Specialized are closed to the entire environment at once, which is what drives the boundary above. Settled by asking for the document and accepting only a document.

Others worth credit: what the manual upload path from SYS-003 to AWS GovCloud actually transfers and who performs it; whether the ClearPath technicians hold individual accounts or a shared one; whether the Huntsville workstations store CUI locally or work over the tunnel; what the M365 GCC High Conditional Access exclusions contain; and whether Acuitas is best treated as a subcontractor, an ESP, or both.

An open item is not an admission of failure. Naming precisely what you could not determine, why it matters, and what evidence would settle it is stronger work than a confident answer that rests on an assumption.
