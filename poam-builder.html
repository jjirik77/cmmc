POA&M Builder, Closeout, and Sustainment

Eligibility first: the 0.8 threshold, then the six that may never be deferred. Then milestones, owners, and dates inside the window — and what the Affirming Official is carrying.

§170.216 never-defer180-day windowPrints as D9

The rules, before you build anything

RuleWhat it means
The score thresholdA POA&M may be used only where the assessment score divided by the total number of Level 2 requirements is at least 0.8. For 110 requirements that is 88. Below that there is no plan of action available at all — the answer is no issuance.
The six that may never be deferredAC.L2-3.1.20 · AC.L2-3.1.22 · CA.L2-3.12.4 · PE.L2-3.10.3 · PE.L2-3.10.4 · PE.L2-3.10.5. These must be MET.
The encryption exceptionSC.L2-3.13.11 may be placed on a POA&M only where encryption is employed but is not FIPS validated. Where no encryption is employed, it may not.
The window180 days from the Conditional CMMC Status Date, closed out by a closeout assessment. Miss it and the conditional status expires.
Who closes it outAn authorized or accredited C3PAO — and it need not be the firm that ran Phases 1 through 3. It assumes responsibility for the Final status determination after its own conflict-of-interest review.

Notice what the point values do not tell you

CA.L2-3.12.4 — the System Security Plan — is worth one point. PE.L2-3.10.3 — escorting visitors — is worth one point. Both are on the never-defer list.

A company can be one point from a perfect score and still be unable to obtain any status at all. Scoring weight measures exploitability; POA&M eligibility measures something else entirely, and conflating the two is the most expensive mistake available in this area.

Throughout: every deferral justified against §170.21 by requirement identifier. “Low risk” is not a basis for deferral; eligibility is. And no remediation design — naming what must be remediated and by when is the plan; specifying how is consulting, and it is still consulting on the last afternoon.

1

Part A — Eligibility, for Meridian as you assessed it

Work from your own findings and your own score. You will reach the conclusion quickly.

Model answer

Not eligible, on two independent grounds.

The threshold. An assessed score around 44 gives 0.40 — half the 0.8 threshold. And 44 is an optimistic ceiling, since it derives from a thirteen-item gap list rather than an assessment of all 110 requirements.

The enumerated list. Even at a score above 88 it would fail, because two of the six requirements that may never be deferred are NOT MET: CA.L2-3.12.4 and PE.L2-3.10.3. These bind independently of the score.

Status: none. No Conditional and no Final CMMC Status may issue.

Saying the consequence out loud. A determination of no issuance is not the end of the sentence. Meridian must win a AMCOM recompete that requires a current assessment of record it does not have. It is in breach today of the DFARS 252.204-7012 clause in the contracts it already holds — an obligation that exists independently of CMMC entirely. And its chief executive has already affirmed a score of 88 in SPRS, in a federal system, under penalty, on the basis that other people told her the number was right.

Assessors do not decide what happens to companies. They do have to be able to say plainly what their determination means, without softening it and without enjoying it.

2

AC.L2-3.1.5 — Employ the principle of least privilege

3 points · NOT MET in the revised assessment.

Model answer

May be deferred. Deferrable. Not on the enumerated list, and the score threshold is met.

3

AU.L2-3.3.2 — Ensure actions can be traced to individual users

3 points · NOT MET in the revised assessment.

Model answer

May be deferred. Deferrable. Note the dependency: actions cannot be traced to individuals while a shared Global Admin service account and shared MSP credentials remain, so sequence it after 3.1.5.

4

CM.L2-3.4.1 — Establish and maintain baseline configurations and inventories

5 points · NOT MET in the revised assessment.

Model answer

May be deferred. Deferrable, and the heaviest item at 5 points. It is also a dependency for several others — you cannot enforce a configuration you have not defined, and you cannot categorize assets you have not inventoried. Sequence it first.

5

MA.L2-3.7.5 — MFA for nonlocal maintenance sessions; terminate them on completion

5 points · NOT MET in the revised assessment.

Model answer

May be deferred. Deferrable. Depends on a contractual change with ClearPath as well as a technical one, which is worth reflecting in the milestone and the owner — this is the one item where Contracts, not IT, holds the critical path.

6

CA.L2-3.12.4 — Develop, document, and periodically update the System Security Plan

1 point · NOT MET in the revised assessment.

Model answer

May never be deferred. May never be deferred. One of the six enumerated in §170.21. Worth one point and an absolute barrier — it must be MET before any status can issue.

7

PE.L2-3.10.3 — Escort visitors and monitor visitor activity

1 point · NOT MET in the revised assessment.

Model answer

May never be deferred. May never be deferred. Also one of the six, also worth one point. The Dayton escort policy for MSP technicians is informal and undocumented, and Huntsville has no visitor control Meridian operates at all.

8

The conclusion that follows

The revised assessment

Nine months later Meridian engages a closeout-capable C3PAO for a fresh Level 2 certification assessment. This time the company has implemented rather than documented. Every requirement is MET except the six above. Total deductions 18. Assessment score 92. 92 ÷ 110 = 0.836.

Model answer

The score alone: yes. 0.836 clears 0.8 comfortably, and a company reading only the threshold rule would expect a Conditional status.

Every requirement on the plan: no. CA.L2-3.12.4 and PE.L2-3.10.3 are on the never-defer list — and they are the two cheapest items on the sheet, at one point each.

What may issue: nothing yet. Both ineligible requirements must be brought to MET before any status can issue. Once they are, the remaining four — AC.L2-3.1.5, AU.L2-3.3.2, CM.L2-3.4.1, and MP.L2-3.8.7, totalling 16 points against a score that would then stand at 94 — may go on a POA&M supporting a Conditional Level 2 status, closed out within 180 days of the Conditional CMMC Status Date.

The lesson, stated plainly: the score alone never answers the question. Work the eligibility column before anything else — it determines whether the rest of the row exists.

Sequencing worth credit: CM.L2-3.4.1 first, because baseline configurations are a dependency for enforcing least privilege; AU.L2-3.3.2 after AC.L2-3.1.5, because tracing actions to individuals is not achievable while shared service accounts persist.

9

Part C — Closeout and sustainment

How the conditional status becomes final, and what carries afterward.

Model answer

Window180 days from the Conditional CMMC Status Date.
CoverageThe requirements on the POA&M, assessed to confirm they are now MET.
Who may conduct itAn authorized or accredited C3PAO — and it may be a different firm from the one that ran Phases 1–3.
What that firm must do firstIts own conflict-of-interest review. It then assumes responsibility for the Final status determination and issues the Final certificate.
On expiryThe Conditional status expires.
Certification periodThree years from the CMMC Status Date.
Annual affirmationSubmitted in SPRS by the Affirming Official — a senior official of the OSC with authority to bind it. Not the IT manager, not the consultant, not the assessor.
What it attestsThat the organization continues to meet the requirements. A present-tense statement, not a memory of the assessment.
The exposureAn affirmation that is not true is a false statement in a federal system, with False Claims Act consequences attaching personally to the official who signed.

On Ellison. She affirmed 88 because Dennis and the consultant said that was the number. She did not verify it and would not know how, and she does not know what an Affirming Official is personally liable for. Nobody at Meridian behaved dishonestly. The number is still wrong, and she is still the person who attested to it in a federal system.

What you say is what is true: what the affirmation is, what it attests, who carries it, and that a submitted score which overstates the position is a false statement. What you do not say is what she should do about it. That remains consulting, and it remains consulting on the last afternoon of the week.

The practical answer to sustainment — and the reason the question matters beyond this case — is continuous monitoring, so that the affirmation reports something known rather than something assumed.
