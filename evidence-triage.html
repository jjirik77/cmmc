Evidence Triage — Adequate, Sufficient, Both, or Neither

Five artifacts exactly as Meridian supplied them. Name the objective, render the verdict, and say what would change it — then build a request list an OSC could satisfy without asking a question.

5 artifacts15 requirementsRequest listPrints as D5

The two tests

AdequacySufficiency
The questionDoes this evidence exist, and does it match what the requirement intends?Does it show the control is implemented, consistently followed, and operating?
What to ask next“What is this a document about?”“How would I know this is still true, and true everywhere?”

Adequate evidence shows the thing exists and matches what the requirement intends. Sufficient evidence shows it is implemented, consistently followed, and operating. A policy requiring MFA is adequate. A configuration export plus a sample of enrolled accounts is sufficient. Someone telling you it is enforced is neither.

Every verdict needs a fourth field

Most assessors reach similar verdicts on these five artifacts. What separates them is what would change it. An assessor who can say “this is adequate but not sufficient, and here is precisely what would make it sufficient” has given an organization something it can act on without ever telling it what to do. This tool will not let you skip that field.

Artifact 1 — Access Control Policy (POL-001 v1.2)

Supplied by Diana Kowalczyk, CISO, as a PDF. Document register shows last reviewed January 2024 — one of the four policies the company classifies as current.

MERIDIAN PRECISION DEFENSE SYSTEMS - ACCESS CONTROL POLICY
POL-001  v1.2   Last reviewed: January 2024   Approved: M. Ellison

4.1  Access to MPDS information systems shall be granted on the basis of
     least privilege and documented business need.

4.2  Accounts shall be provisioned by IT following an approved HR ticket
     and shall be disabled upon separation.

4.3  Privileged and administrative accounts shall be restricted to
     designated IT personnel and reviewed quarterly.

4.4  Remote access shall require multifactor authentication.

4.5  Third-party administrative access shall be governed by written
     agreement and subject to the same controls as employee access.
Incomplete. A verdict without a “what would change it” clause is not finished. Fill that field before you move on.

Model answer

Adequate. Not sufficient. Offered against AC.L2-3.1.1, 3.1.2, 3.1.5 and IA.L2-3.5.3.

Adequate, and genuinely well drafted — it is current, approved by the CEO, and clauses 4.4 and 4.5 address exactly the two things Meridian gets wrong. That is worth saying out loud.

Not sufficient, and the policy indicts the environment rather than defending it. Clause 4.4 requires MFA for remote access; the SSL VPN has none. Clause 4.5 requires third-party administrative access to be governed by written agreement; ClearPath holds standing domain admin on a month-to-month arrangement with no cybersecurity terms. Clause 4.2 requires disablement on separation; six VPN accounts belong to separated employees. Clause 4.3 requires quarterly privileged-account review; no review record exists, and a shared service account holds Global Admin in the GCC High tenant with no individual owner.

What would change it: an account listing with privilege levels reconciled against a documented baseline; dated quarterly review records naming the reviewer; termination records showing account disablement timestamps; the executed ClearPath agreement with security terms; and MFA evidence at the network access layer.

The observation that marks a strong assessor: a policy this good makes the findings worse, not better. Meridian wrote down the right rule and did not follow it, which forecloses the “we did not know” reading and speaks directly to whether the control is operating.

Artifact 2 — M365 GCC High Conditional Access export

Supplied by Trevor Nakamura as a policy export from the GCC High tenant. Tenant identifier visible and matching. Exported three days before the request.

Microsoft 365 GCC High - Conditional Access policy export
Policy:        MPDS-Require-MFA-AllUsers
State:         On
Assignments
  Users:       All users (163)
  Excluded:    mpds-admin@meridianpds.onmicrosoft.com  [service account]
  Cloud apps:  All cloud apps
  Conditions:  Any location
Access controls
  Grant:       Require multifactor authentication
Modified:      7 months ago
Incomplete. A verdict without a “what would change it” clause is not finished. Fill that field before you move on.

Model answer

The hardest artifact in the set, and it fails for two separate reasons. Offered against IA.L2-3.5.3.

Reason one — scope. This is a genuine policy export rather than a screenshot, it is current, and it covers all 163 accounts. And it covers the Microsoft 365 layer only. IA.L2-3.5.3 requires MFA for network access, and the FortiGate SSL VPN — the actual network access path, with roughly 22 users including the MSP — authenticates against Active Directory with username and password alone. Evidence for the cloud layer does not establish the objective for the network layer.

Reason two — the exclusion. Unlike a screenshot, an export shows exclusions, and this one excludes mpds-admin@: a shared service account holding Global Admin with no individual owner. The single most privileged identity in the tenant is exempted from the MFA policy. A team that reads the first four lines and stops will miss it, which is precisely why the export is the better artifact and precisely why you read all of it.

What would change it: MFA evidence at the VPN gateway; a sign-in test against the VPN, the domain controllers and the file server with a sample account; and either removal of the exclusion or a documented, assessed compensating control with an accountable owner for that account.

Hold the distinction: the artifact is excellent evidence and the requirement is NOT MET. An assessor who cannot praise good evidence and still find the requirement unmet will be argued out of findings.

Artifact 3 — Email from the IT Administrator

Supplied by Trevor Nakamura by email, in response to a request for audit log review records.

From:    Trevor Nakamura
To:      Assessment Team
Subject: RE: Evidence request - audit logging

Logging is on across the board - Windows event logs on the DCs and the
file server, and the PLM box writes its own logs. Retention is 30 days,
which is what the platform defaults to. I look at them when something
comes up and we have not had anything unusual. Snort would catch
anything serious anyway.

Happy to pull specific logs if you tell me what you need.

Trevor
Incomplete. A verdict without a “what would change it” clause is not finished. Fill that field before you move on.

Model answer

Neither. Offered against AU.L2-3.3.1 and 3.3.5.

It is an assertion that arrived by email, which does not make it a record. An interview response is a lead to be corroborated, not a conclusion, and this is an interview response in written form.

Three moves worth naming to students explicitly. “I look at them when something comes up” is reactive review, which is not the review the requirement contemplates. “We have not had anything unusual” is an appeal to the absence of evidence — with no defined review process, no correlation and no SIEM, Meridian has no capability to have seen anything unusual. And “Snort would catch anything serious” substitutes a tool’s existence for a process: nobody has said who reads its output, or when.

The detail a strong assessor pursues: 30 days is stated plainly and volunteered as a platform default rather than a decision. The requirement is retention sufficient to support monitoring, analysis, investigation and reporting — a period nobody chose cannot have been assessed against that standard. Ask what analysis established 30 days as sufficient. Nothing did.

What would change it: a documented log review procedure with a defined cadence; dated review records naming the reviewer and the sources reviewed; a retention determination tied to the requirement; and, for 3.3.5, evidence that records from different sources are actually correlated.

Watch for: this is the artifact most likely to be accepted, because Nakamura is competent, cooperative and almost certainly telling the truth as he understands it. Competence and honesty are not evidence, and the assessments that go wrong go wrong with likeable people.

Supplied by Diana Kowalczyk. The document register records it as last performed June 2023 and flags it “Overdue.” Six risks are recorded.

MERIDIAN PRECISION DEFENSE SYSTEMS - RISK REGISTER (extract)
Performed: June 2023          Next review: not scheduled

ID       Risk                                  L    I    Level     Remediation            Owner
RISK-01  Unpatched ENG workstations exploited  High High Critical  Planned - no date      Nakamura
RISK-02  MSP privileged access abused          Med  High High      Not initiated          Kowalczyk
RISK-03  Stale VPN accounts used by former     Med  High High      Partial - HR notified  Nakamura
         employee or attacker
RISK-04  CUI exfiltrated via removable media   Med  Med  Medium    Policy only            Kowalczyk
RISK-05  Fire/flood in Dayton server room -    Low  High Medium    Local NAS only         Nakamura
         no offsite backup for SYS-003
RISK-06  Huntsville closet equipment accessed  Low  Med  Low-Med   Not addressed          Farris
Incomplete. A verdict without a “what would change it” clause is not finished. Fill that field before you move on.

Model answer

Adequate. Not sufficient. Offered against RA.L2-3.11.1.

Adequate: unlike most artifacts in this case it is the right kind of document, it identifies real risks specific to this environment, and it assigns likelihood, impact and an owner. Meridian has genuinely done a risk assessment.

Not sufficient: the requirement is to assess risk periodically. This was performed once, 19 months ago, with no next review scheduled — so there is no periodicity to evidence. Two of the six risks record no remediation at all, one records “planned” with no date, and there is no evidence that any of them were tracked, escalated or closed in the intervening period.

The observation that separates Competent from Assessor-Ready: read the register against the assessment findings. RISK-01, RISK-02 and RISK-03 describe, precisely and in the company’s own words, three of the failures you are about to write up as findings. Meridian identified them, rated them Critical and High, assigned owners, and then did nothing for 19 months. That is not an unknown risk. It is an accepted one that nobody was authorised to accept — and it goes directly to whether management is exercising the oversight the CA family requires.

What would change it: a defined assessment cadence with evidence of at least two cycles; remediation tracking showing movement on the open items; and evidence of management review and formal risk acceptance where remediation was deliberately deferred.

Artifact 5 — The POA&M

Supplied by Diana Kowalczyk as an .xlsx attachment. Asked where the master copy lives, she states it is in her OneDrive.

MPDS_POAM_working.xlsx
Location:  Kowalczyk personal OneDrive (commercial tenant)
           NOT the M365 GCC High tenant

Item  Weakness                          Target    Status      Owner
1     VPN MFA                           TBD       Open        IT
2     Stale accounts                    TBD       In progress IT
3     Log review process                -         Open        IT
4     Workstation patching              Q3?       Open        IT
5     MSP contract / SCRM               -         Open        Contracts
6     Segmentation project              FY26      Open        IT

No version history. No AO signature. Last modified 18 months ago.
Incomplete. A verdict without a “what would change it” clause is not finished. Fill that field before you move on.

Model answer

Neither — and the artifact is itself a finding. Offered against CA.L2-3.12.2 and, as supporting evidence, CA.L2-3.12.4.

Not adequate: a plan of action must identify the weakness, the corrective action, the resources required, the milestones, and the completion dates. Four of six items carry no target date, none carries a named individual owner, none records resources, and no item has ever been closed. “IT” is not an owner. This does not match what the requirement intends.

Not sufficient for the obvious reason and a much worse one: it is stored outside the CUI boundary. The master copy lives in a personal OneDrive on a commercial tenant, not in the authorised GCC High environment. A document enumerating every unremediated security weakness in a defense contractor is precisely the kind of information an adversary would target, and it is being kept outside the accredited boundary.

Do not stop at the content. Where an artifact lives can be a more serious finding than what it says, and this is the clearest example in the case. It also contradicts the SSP’s own boundary statement, which is a second finding.

What would change it: a POA&M inside the GCC High boundary, under version control, with named individual owners, resource estimates, dated milestones, and evidence of AO review at a defined cadence.

Consistency check: you will need this document again on Day 5, when the eligibility rules in 32 CFR §170.21 turn on what a POA&M may and may not carry. Note now that a POA&M is a regulated instrument with a defined content standard, not a spreadsheet of intentions.

Part B — the evidence request list

Build request lines for the fifteen requirements above. Each line names four things: the artifact (specific enough to be retrieved), the method (examine, interview, or test), the objective it serves, and the custodian.

A line that names a topic rather than an artifact is not a request. “Access control documentation” produces a pile of documents and a week of delay; “the current Conditional Access policy export from the Microsoft 365 tenant, including exclusions” does not.

Model answer

What good looks like — a sample of strong lines:

  • FortiGate SSL VPN authentication configuration export showing the authentication method for the remote-access portal · Examine · IA.L2-3.5.3 · T. Nakamura — and a live sign-in test to the VPN, a domain controller over RDP, and SYS-003 · Test · same objective · T. Nakamura.
  • Active Directory account listing with group memberships, last-logon dates and enabled/disabled state, exported during the assessment · Examine · AC.L2-3.1.1, 3.1.5 · T. Nakamura.
  • Separation records for the six accounts identified as belonging to former employees, with the corresponding disablement timestamps · Examine · AC.L2-3.1.1 · R. Bauer and T. Nakamura — note the two custodians; that split is itself informative about why the gap exists.
  • The executed ClearPath IT Solutions services agreement including any security addendum, plus the Customer Responsibility Matrix · Examine · MA.L2-3.7.5 and the ESP obligation · P. Mehta.
  • Log retention configuration for the domain controllers, SYS-003 and SYS-004, showing the configured period; and any documented analysis establishing that period as sufficient · Examine · AU.L2-3.3.1 · T. Nakamura.
  • Patch status report for all 14 Dayton and 6 Huntsville engineering workstations as at the assessment date · Examine · CM.L2-3.4.2 · T. Nakamura.
  • Disposition records for the three decommissioned drives held in the Dayton server room, and the destruction certificates for media disposed of since 2022 · Examine · MP.L2-3.8.3 · G. Farris.
  • Any record of an incident response exercise since 2022, and the current IR contact roster reconciled against active personnel · Examine · IR.L2-3.6.1, 3.6.3 · D. Kowalczyk.

Three checks before you submit: every line names an artifact somebody could go and fetch, not a subject somebody could write an essay about. Every verdict names the objective. And nothing anywhere in the document tells Meridian what to do about any of it.

The habit that matters most: ask for what would disconfirm the company’s position, not only what would confirm it. Meridian has an SSP, ten policies and a CISO — a documentation-led assessment here will pass a company that is not compliant.
