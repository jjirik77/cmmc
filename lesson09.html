Day 5 · Afternoon · CAP Phase 4 · Transfer test · Closes the CCP blueprint

POA&M, Closeout, the Transfer Test, and What You Do Next

This morning you told Meridian it does not get a certificate. Now you deal with what happens next — for the company, for the regulatory environment you will work in, and for you. Then you are handed a company you have never seen and given thirty minutes, because that is the only honest measure of whether this week gave you a method or a familiarity.

Day 5 — PM ~3 hours Cold transfer test Tool: POA&M Builder

Regulatory status as of 16 November 2026: Phase 2 is suspended; check again before you teach.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to…

  • Construct a POA&M consistent with 32 CFR §170.21, distinguishing what may be deferred from what may never be. Bloom: Create
  • Explain Conditional versus Final Level 2 status, the 180-day closeout window, and what happens when it expires. Bloom: Understand
  • Describe the three-year certification period, the annual affirmation, and what the Affirming Official is exposed to. Bloom: Understand
  • Evaluate a change in CMMC policy against primary sources, separating what is stated from what is inferred and what is advocated. Bloom: Evaluate
  • Scope a previously unseen environment under time constraint. Bloom: Apply
  • Produce a credential roadmap with dated milestones toward CCP and CCA. Bloom: Create

1

What a POA&M can and cannot do

The fact that governs this session

A plan of action is not a way to defer anything you like. Six requirements may never be placed on a POA&M at all, and a POA&M may not be used at any level unless the assessment score is at least 0.8 of the total.

Two of the six are worth one point each. The cheapest requirements on the sheet are absolute barriers, and no amount of score makes them go away.

RuleWhat it means in practice
The score thresholdA POA&M may be used only where the assessment score divided by the total number of Level 2 requirements is at least 0.8. For 110 requirements that is 88. Below that there is no plan of action available at all — the answer is no issuance.
The six that may never be deferredAC.L2-3.1.20, AC.L2-3.1.22, CA.L2-3.12.4, PE.L2-3.10.3, PE.L2-3.10.4, PE.L2-3.10.5. These must be MET.
The encryption exceptionSC.L2-3.13.11 may be placed on a POA&M only where encryption is employed but is not FIPS validated. Where no encryption is employed, it may not.
The window180 days from the Conditional CMMC Status Date, closed out by a closeout assessment. Miss it and the conditional status expires.
Who closes it outAn authorized or accredited C3PAO — and it need not be the firm that ran Phases 1 through 3. A closeout C3PAO assumes responsibility for the Final status determination and issues the Final certificate, after its own conflict-of-interest review.
Notice what the point values do not tell you

CA.L2-3.12.4 — the System Security Plan — is worth one point. PE.L2-3.10.3 — escorting visitors — is worth one point. Both are on the list that may never be deferred.

A company can be one point from a perfect score and still be unable to obtain any status at all. Scoring weight measures exploitability; POA&M eligibility measures something else entirely, and conflating the two is the most expensive mistake available in this area.

2

Meridian, as you assessed it

Work Part A from your own findings, not from a supplied list. You will reach the conclusion quickly.

Meridian's assessed score does not approach 88. And two of the six requirements that may never be deferred are already failing: the System Security Plan requirement, and PE.L2-3.10.3 — the Huntsville warehouse has no visitor log, no badge access, and delivery drivers come and go.

Meridian is not POA&M-eligible. No CMMC status may issue.

Say the consequence out loud

A determination of no issuance is not the end of the sentence. Write two or three lines on what it means commercially: the AMCOM recompete the company must win to survive; the clause in the contract it already holds and is in breach of today; and the affirmation of a score of 88 that its chief executive has already signed, in a federal system, under penalty.

Assessors do not decide what happens to companies. They do have to be able to say plainly what their determination means — without softening it, and without enjoying it.

3

Building a POA&M that works

Building a plan is a skill you need, so Part B works a revised assessment. Nine months later Meridian engages a closeout-capable C3PAO for a fresh Level 2 certification assessment. This time the company has implemented rather than documented. Every requirement is MET except six.

RequirementShort titlePointsDeductionDeferrable?

MP.L2-3.8.7Control the use of removable media55Yes
CA.L2-3.12.4Develop, document, and periodically update the SSP11Never
PE.L2-3.10.3Escort visitors and monitor visitor activity11Never
Total deductions18
Assessment score9292 ÷ 110 = 0.836
The trap, and it is worth meeting here rather than in the field

The score clears the threshold. 0.836 is comfortably above 0.8, and a company reading only the threshold rule would expect a Conditional status.

It cannot have one. Two of the six NOT MET requirements are on the never-defer list, and they are the two cheapest items on the sheet. Both must be MET outright before any status can issue. The score alone never answers the question.

For each deferrable item the plan needs a milestone, a target date inside the window, and an owner. A milestone with no owner is a wish. Sequence them so dependencies are visible — baseline configurations before least privilege, for instance, because you cannot enforce what you have not defined.

And no remediation design. Naming what must be remediated and by when is the plan; specifying how to remediate it is consulting, and it is still consulting on the last afternoon.

4

Closeout and sustainment

QuestionAnswer
From what date does the closeout window run, and how long?180 days from the Conditional CMMC Status Date.
What does the closeout assessment cover?The requirements on the POA&M, assessed to confirm they are now MET.
Who may conduct it?An authorized or accredited C3PAO — and it may be a different firm from the one that ran Phases 1–3.
What must that firm do first?Its own conflict-of-interest review. It then assumes responsibility for the Final status determination and issues the Final certificate.
What happens if the window expires?The Conditional status expires.
How long does a Level 2 certification run?Three years from the CMMC Status Date.
What is submitted annually, by whom, where?An affirmation in SPRS by the Affirming Official.
What does it attest?That the organization continues to meet the requirements. Present tense, not a memory of the assessment.
What is the Affirming Official exposed to?False Claims Act consequences attaching personally, where the affirmation is not true.

The practical answer to the sustainment problem is continuous monitoring, so that the affirmation reports something known rather than something assumed. Marcus Ellison affirmed a score of 88 on the basis that other people said it was right. Hold that in mind when you write this section.

5

Reading a policy change against primary sources

This is the Day 1 exercise returning at full strength. Then you sorted statements from a single alert into fact, inference, and advice. Now you do it across three sources that do not agree with one another, on a subject where the primary sources are incomplete and moving.

  • Two professional analyses of the same recent CMMC development, from firms that reach different conclusions.
  • A government or trade-body notice on the same subject — a different kind of source with a different agenda.

Mark every substantive sentence as stated, inferred, or advocated. Then the harder question: where the two firms disagree, what would settle it — and is that thing published, pending, or unknowable?

Why this is the last analytical exercise of the workshop

Everything else you have learned has a shelf life. This is the skill that lets you notice when it expires. An assessor who can say what would settle a disagreement, and whether that thing exists yet, can tell a client the difference between a risk and a rumour.

6

The transfer test

Thirty minutes. Individual. No notes, no case study, no internet. You receive a profile of a company in Meridian's supply chain and produce a scope determination.

It is not graded and it does not affect completion. It exists to tell you something you cannot find out any other way: whether this week gave you a method or a familiarity. Both feel identical from the inside, right up until you meet a new environment.

Acuitas Machining Inc.

Springfield, Ohio, about 25 miles from Meridian’s Dayton plant. Fourteen people. Precision CNC machining and inspection — a job shop taking overflow and short-run work. About $800K of its $3.4M revenue comes from a single relationship: machining overflow on Meridian’s actuator housing line for the AMCOM contract.

No server. No domain. No IT staff. No SSP, no asset inventory, no network diagram, no written security policy of any kind, and no record of any security decision ever having been made.

The owner's position, stated plainly: “CMMC is a DoD thing — we don't have a contract with the government, we have a contract with Meridian.”

What you must produce

  1. Level and authority (~4 min). What level applies and what makes it apply? Answer the owner's objection directly, in no more than four sentences, and name the instrument you rely on.
  2. Asset categorization (~14 min). Eight assets into the five categories, one-line basis each. Where a category is unavailable to Acuitas, say so and say why.
  3. The consequential unknown (~4 min). The single fact you would most want resolved before finalizing this scope, why it matters, and what evidence would settle it.
What is being measured

Not recall. Whether the method you have used on one company for four days works on a company you have never seen — which is the only thing that would make you useful to the next OSC that hires you.

A confident wrong categorization scores worse than a correctly hedged one. If a category is genuinely arguable, say what makes it arguable and what would settle it. That is the assessor's answer.

8

The Meridian case

Meridian · Day 5 Afternoon · Deliverable D9 + Transfer Test · Fictional case

Close it out, then prove the method transfers

The POA&M Builder runs Parts A to C. Part A takes your own assessed score and findings and walks the eligibility test — the threshold, then the never-defer list — and asks you to state the commercial consequence in your own words. Part B loads the revised assessment at 92 and lets you build the plan item by item; the tool refuses a deferral of CA.L2-3.12.4 or PE.L2-3.10.3 and tells you why. Part C is closeout and sustainment.

Then the Acuitas transfer test opens cold, on a thirty-minute timer, with no access to the Meridian material. Eight assets, three questions. When the timer stops, the determination key opens — including the honest account of where the test is won or lost, which is the industrial printer controller.

Nothing anywhere in D9 tells Meridian how to remediate anything. Naming what must be done and by when is the plan. Designing it is somebody else's job — and after this week, never yours on a company you assess.

The facilitation decisions that matter

SessionThe momentHow to run it
L1Students raise whatever is currently in the trade press about CMMC changing Answer it on day one using the instrument test, not the news: has a class deviation, a FAR/DFARS rule change, or an amendment to Part 170 issued? If not, the framework is what the published text says. Then redirect. Do not let it recur — and note that you have just modelled the L9 skill in thirty seconds.

Students uncomfortable ending on an open item are learning the correct discomfort.
L3Ethics scenario adjudication One of the eight scenarios is not addressed directly by the CoPC at all. Finding that out is the exercise. An honest “the document does not cover this, so here is what governs” is a stronger answer than a citation that does not say what the student claims.
L4The conditional categories Teach the test before the name. Do the two conditional categories last and slowly: read the condition aloud, then ask what Meridian would show you. The silence is the lesson.
L5Artifact 2, the Conditional Access export The artifact that separates Competent from Assessor-Ready. Watch for students who accept it outright because it is real technical evidence, and students who reject it outright because Meridian fails the requirement. L6Cross-presentation Require teams to lead with their least comfortable determination. Teams instinctively present their best work, which wastes the exercise — nobody learns from a determination everyone agrees with.

What must be withheld

Three cold instruments

The Day 5 evidence package. Issued at the in-brief. A team that has read the nine documents in advance has done a different exercise, and the time pressure that makes the mock assessment realistic disappears.

The interview role cards. Role-players only, one card each, never shown to assessment teams. Every card contains at least one fact that contradicts something in the evidence package, and those contradictions are the assessable content.

The Acuitas transfer test. Do not release the profile in advance, do not preview the company, and do not answer questions about it once the clock starts.

How to play the interview cards

The rule that makes it work: answer the question you were asked, and nothing more. Each card has material that is volunteered freely and material that emerges only if a student asks a question that reaches it — a real question, not a yes-or-no invitation to agree.

If a student asks “you review the logs regularly, correct?”, say yes. That is a true answer to a bad question, and the student learning why it was a bad question is the point of the exercise. If a team asks only closed questions, let the interview end early and uselessly. The silence afterwards teaches more than a rescue would.

Hold the contradictions. If a team leaves without finding any of them, that is the finding — and it is the one to open the debrief with.

Assessing competency rather than grading

Four levels, applied to every deliverable. Competent is the completion standard; Assessor-Ready describes work that would survive professional challenge.

LevelWhat it looks like across the deliverables
Not YetAdopts the contractor's own claims wholesale. Categorizes by intuition or by what an asset is for. Determines at the requirement level. Recommends remediation.
DevelopingHandles the obvious cases and goes quiet on the ambiguous ones. Applies one test but not both. Names a requirement but not the objective. Advice appears once, in passing.
CompetentEvery determination evidenced and traceable. Conditional categories recognised as earned. Both adequacy and sufficiency applied. No advice anywhere; gaps named without fixes.
Assessor-ReadySays what would change each answer. Catches the under-classified item and explains why that direction is more dangerous. Names a gap so precisely that the fix is obvious without ever being stated. Written so that a company that dislikes the result still recognises it as fair.
The single most useful marking habit

Look for the what would change it clause. A determination that cannot describe its own falsifying evidence has not been reasoned — it has been asserted. That one test separates Competent from Developing more reliably than any other, and it is fast to apply.

Maintaining this material

Standing obligation before every run

Re-verify the regulatory position within seven days of any workshop start date. This material deliberately carries no dated policy layer — it teaches 32 CFR Part 170 and the DFARS clause family as published, so that it does not go stale with the news cycle. That design decision only holds if you check the published instruments before you teach.

Practical notes

  • Minimum viable cohort is twelve — four teams of three — for the split-case design. Below that, run a single case per session and extend the challenge rounds.
  • Schedule the Day 5 sessions contiguously. The overnight evidence-request bridge only reads as overnight if there is a break; if you must compress, run the request as a 15-minute in-session gap and say so.
  • No purchased software, lab environment, or clearance is required. Every artifact is fictional and unclassified. Every primary source is free and publicly available.
  • Note on dodcio.defense.gov: that host blocks automated tools but opens normally in a browser. Do not report the links as dead.
  • Where a portal module disagrees with this material on who issues the CCP, this material is right and the portal predates December 2025. Use the discrepancy in L0 — it is the cleanest available demonstration of source authority.

Knowledge Check

Test your judgment

Ten questions in CCP style. Read every rationale.

Q1.An OSC scores 92 of 110 (0.836). Its NOT MET requirements include CA.L2-3.12.4 and PE.L2-3.10.3. What status may issue?

Question 1 options
Correct: D. Both must be MET outright before any status can issue. They are the two cheapest items on the sheet, at one point each. The score alone never answers the question. Why the others fall short: A The trap this session exists to spring. Clearing the threshold is necessary and not sufficient.; B Final status requires all requirements MET, and these are not.; C There is no exception mechanism. The list is absolute.

Q2.Which requirement may be placed on a POA&M only in a specific circumstance?

Question 2 options
Correct: B. Where no encryption is employed at all, it may not be deferred. Note the symmetry with the scoring exception: the same distinction drives both the 3-versus-5 deduction and POA&M eligibility. Why the others fall short: A 3.12.4 may never be placed on a POA&M in any circumstance.; C On the never-defer list, and tenancy is irrelevant.; D Also on the never-defer list.

Q3.From what date does the 180-day closeout window run?

Question 3 options
Correct: D. 180 days from that date, closed out by a closeout assessment. Miss it and the conditional status expires. Why the others fall short: A The out-brief is a Phase 3 event and does not start the clock.; B Submission is not the trigger.; C No such acceptance step exists in the closeout mechanics.

Q4.Who may conduct the closeout assessment?

Question 4 options
Correct: C. The closeout firm assumes responsibility for the Final status determination and issues the Final certificate. Its conflict review is its own, not inherited. Why the others fall short: A A common assumption and the rule is explicitly the opposite.; B DIBCAC conducts government-led assessments, including Level 3. Closeout of a Level 2 conditional status is C3PAO work.; D A conditional status arising from a certification assessment cannot be closed out by self-assessment.

Q5.Acuitas's owner says CMMC does not apply because he has no contract with the Government. What is the complete answer?

Question 5 options
Correct: B. The obligation follows the information, not the tier. The clause is in his subcontract, flowdown is how it got there, Meridian was required to put it there, and whether he has read it is irrelevant to whether it binds him. Why the others fall short: A This concedes the owner's premise. The obligation is not a commercial courtesy.; C The most serious error available on this test, and it is the owner's own error. Tier does not determine level; information does.; D The right conclusion without the authority. Naming the flowdown mechanism is what makes it an answer.

Q6.Acuitas's three CNC machining centres have embedded Windows controllers that retain drawing-derived files for months. How are they categorized?

Question 6 options
Correct: A. Where the test is won or lost. The category exists and Acuitas cannot claim it: no inventory, no SSP, no network diagram, no policy of any kind. Knowing the rule and its condition is the difference between having read the scoping guide and being able to use it. Why the others fall short: B The weakest answer. They have not been read as information systems, and they retain CUI for months.; C Thorough, and it misses that the Specialized Asset category exists and covers Operational Technology.; D That category carries the same documentation and policy condition, which also fails — and OT is the better-fitting type in any case.

Q7.Acuitas's part-time bookkeeper works from home on her own computer with access to the full shared drive. How is she treated?

Question 7 options
Correct: A. She holds an account with access to CUI and works from her own equipment. No CRM exists. Note the second ESP alongside her: the IT contractor with tenant administrator rights, who handles Security Protection Data. Why the others fall short: B The dismissal the test is designed to catch. Her access is to the full shared drive, which holds the pattern files.; C Categories apply to assets. An external party is analysed under the ESP rule.; D Having an account does not make an external party internal. The ESP rule exists precisely for this.

Q8.At Acuitas, what should be scoped out?

Question 8 options
Correct: B. Out-of-Scope requires the contractor to be prepared to justify the asset's inability to process, store, or transmit CUI. The absence of segmentation is what makes that impossible, and saying so explicitly is the correct answer. Why the others fall short: A It holds inspection programs written from the Meridian drawings and generates reports referencing them. Students who scope the machine tools carefully and forget the metrology station have found the obvious OT and missed the quieter one.; C Folders are not assets, and they sit on a shared drive every account can read.; D It opens pattern files. It is a CUI Asset, and the shared login with the password on the monitor is a finding rather than a scoping question.

Q9.Two law firm analyses of the same CMMC development reach different conclusions. What is the assessor's most useful next question?

Question 9 options
Correct: C. The question that distinguishes a risk from a rumour. An assessor who can answer it can tell a client which of the two they are looking at. Why the others fall short: A Authority of the author is not how a disagreement about a primary source is settled.; B Conservatism is a posture, not an analysis, and it can be wrong in either direction.; D Useful input, and it does not resolve a disagreement about what the existing instruments mean.

Q10.What does the annual affirmation attest, and who may make it?

Question 10 options
Correct: D. A present-tense statement, not a memory of the assessment. The practical answer is continuous monitoring, so that it reports something known rather than assumed. False Claims Act exposure attaches personally to the signer. Why the others fall short: A The affirmation is the OSC's statement about itself, not the assessor's about the assessment.; B Wrong on both counts. Incidents are reported under a separate clause, and the IT Manager cannot bind the organization.; C Closeout is a separate mechanism with its own assessment, and the C3PAO does not affirm on the OSC's behalf.
