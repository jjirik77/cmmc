1

What a POA&M can and cannot do

The fact that governs this session

A plan of action is not a way to defer anything you like. Six requirements may never be placed on a POA&M at all, and a POA&M may not be used at any level unless the assessment score is at least 0.8 of the total.

Two of the six are worth one point each. The cheapest requirements on the sheet are absolute barriers, and no amount of score makes them go away.

RuleWhat it means in practice
The score thresholdA POA&M may be used only where the assessment score divided by the total number of Level 2 requirements is at least 0.8. For 110 requirements that is 88. Below that there is no plan of action available at all — the answer is no issuance.
The six that may never be deferredAC.L2-3.1.20, AC.L2-3.1.22, CA.L2-3.12.4, PE.L2-3.10.3, PE.L2-3.10.4, PE.L2-3.10.5. These must be MET.
The encryption exceptionSC.L2-3.13.11 may be placed on a POA&M only where encryption is employed but is not FIPS validated. Where no encryption is employed, it may not.
The window180 days from the Conditional CMMC Status Date, closed out by a closeout assessment. Miss it and the conditional status expires.
Who closes it outAn authorized or accredited C3PAO — and it need not be the firm that ran Phases 1 through 3. A closeout C3PAO assumes responsibility for the Final status determination and issues the Final certificate, after its own conflict-of-interest review.
Notice what the point values do not tell you

CA.L2-3.12.4 — the System Security Plan — is worth one point. PE.L2-3.10.3 — escorting visitors — is worth one point. Both are on the list that may never be deferred.

A company can be one point from a perfect score and still be unable to obtain any status at all. Scoring weight measures exploitability; POA&M eligibility measures something else entirely, and conflating the two is the most expensive mistake available in this area.

2

Meridian, as you assessed it

Work Part A from your own findings, not from a supplied list. You will reach the conclusion quickly.

Meridian's assessed score does not approach 88. And two of the six requirements that may never be deferred are already failing: the System Security Plan requirement, and PE.L2-3.10.3 — the Huntsville warehouse has no visitor log, no badge access, and delivery drivers come and go.

Meridian is not POA&M-eligible. No CMMC status may issue.

Say the consequence out loud

A determination of no issuance is not the end of the sentence. Write two or three lines on what it means commercially: the AMCOM recompete the company must win to survive; the clause in the contract it already holds and is in breach of today; and the affirmation of a score of 88 that its chief executive has already signed, in a federal system, under penalty.

Assessors do not decide what happens to companies. They do have to be able to say plainly what their determination means — without softening it, and without enjoying it.

3

Building a POA&M that works

Building a plan is a skill you need, so Part B works a revised assessment. Nine months later Meridian engages a closeout-capable C3PAO for a fresh Level 2 certification assessment. This time the company has implemented rather than documented. Every requirement is MET except six.

The trap, and it is worth meeting here rather than in the field

The score clears the threshold. 0.836 is comfortably above 0.8, and a company reading only the threshold rule would expect a Conditional status.

It cannot have one. Two of the six NOT MET requirements are on the never-defer list, and they are the two cheapest items on the sheet. Both must be MET outright before any status can issue. The score alone never answers the question.

For each deferrable item the plan needs a milestone, a target date inside the window, and an owner. A milestone with no owner is a wish. Sequence them so dependencies are visible — baseline configurations before least privilege, for instance, because you cannot enforce what you have not defined.

And no remediation design. Naming what must be remediated and by when is the plan; specifying how to remediate it is consulting, and it is still consulting on the last afternoon.

4

Closeout and sustainment

QuestionAnswer
From what date does the closeout window run, and how long?180 days from the Conditional CMMC Status Date.
What does the closeout assessment cover?The requirements on the POA&M, assessed to confirm they are now MET.
Who may conduct it?An authorized or accredited C3PAO — and it may be a different firm from the one that ran Phases 1–3.
What must that firm do first?Its own conflict-of-interest review. It then assumes responsibility for the Final status determination and issues the Final certificate.
What happens if the window expires?The Conditional status expires.
How long does a Level 2 certification run?Three years from the CMMC Status Date.
What is submitted annually, by whom, where?An affirmation in SPRS by the Affirming Official.
What does it attest?That the organization continues to meet the requirements. Present tense, not a memory of the assessment.
What is the Affirming Official exposed to?False Claims Act consequences attaching personally, where the affirmation is not true.

The practical answer to the sustainment problem is continuous monitoring, so that the affirmation reports something known rather than something assumed. Marcus Ellison affirmed a score of 88 on the basis that other people told her it was right. Hold that in mind when you write this section.

5

Reading a policy change against primary sources

This is the Day 1 exercise returning at full strength. Then you sorted statements from a single alert into fact, inference, and advice. Now you do it across three sources that do not agree with one another, on a subject where the primary sources are incomplete and moving.

  • Two professional analyses of the same recent CMMC development, from firms that reach different conclusions.
  • A government or trade-body notice on the same subject — a different kind of source with a different agenda.

Mark every substantive sentence as stated, inferred, or advocated. Then the harder question: where the two firms disagree, what would settle it — and is that thing published, pending, or unknowable?

Why this is the last analytical exercise of the workshop

Everything else you have learned has a shelf life. This is the skill that lets you notice when it expires. An assessor who can say what would settle a disagreement, and whether that thing exists yet, can tell a client the difference between a risk and a rumour.

6

The transfer test

Thirty minutes. Individual. No notes, no case study, no internet. You receive a profile of a company in Meridian's supply chain and produce a scope determination.

It is not graded and it does not affect completion. It exists to tell you something you cannot find out any other way: whether this week gave you a method or a familiarity. Both feel identical from the inside, right up until you meet a new environment.

Acuitas Machining Inc.

Springfield, Ohio, about 25 miles from Meridian’s Dayton plant. Fourteen people. Precision CNC machining and inspection — a job shop taking overflow and short-run work. About $800K of its $3.4M revenue comes from a single relationship: machining overflow on Meridian’s actuator housing line for the AMCOM contract.

No server. No domain. No IT staff. No SSP, no asset inventory, no network diagram, no written security policy of any kind, and no record of any security decision ever having been made.

The owner's position, stated plainly: “CMMC is a DoD thing — we don't have a contract with the government, we have a contract with Meridian.”

What you must produce

  1. Level and authority (~4 min). What level applies and what makes it apply? Answer the owner's objection directly, in no more than four sentences, and name the instrument you rely on.
  2. Asset categorization (~14 min). Eight assets into the five categories, one-line basis each. Where a category is unavailable to Acuitas, say so and say why.
  3. The consequential unknown (~4 min). The single fact you would most want resolved before finalizing this scope, why it matters, and what evidence would settle it.
What is being measured

Not recall. Whether the method you have used on one company for four days works on a company you have never seen — which is the only thing that would make you useful to the next OSC that hires you.

A confident wrong categorization scores worse than a correctly hedged one. If a category is genuinely arguable, say what makes it arguable and what would settle it. That is the assessor's answer.

Meridian — your turn

Meridian · Day 5 Afternoon · Deliverable D9 + Transfer Test

Close it out, then prove the method transfers

The POA&M Builder runs Parts A to C. Part A takes your own assessed score and findings and walks the eligibility test — the threshold, then the never-defer list — and asks you to state the commercial consequence in your own words. Part B loads the revised assessment at 92 and lets you build the plan item by item; the tool refuses a deferral of CA.L2-3.12.4 or PE.L2-3.10.3 and tells you why. Part C is closeout and sustainment.

Then the Acuitas transfer test opens cold, on a thirty-minute timer, with no access to the Meridian material. Eight assets, three questions. When the timer stops, the determination key opens — including the honest account of where the test is won or lost, which is the industrial printer controller.

Nothing anywhere in D9 tells Meridian how to remediate anything. Naming what must be done and by when is the plan. Designing it is somebody else's job — and after this week, never yours on a company you assess.
