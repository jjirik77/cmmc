Scoping and Asset Categorization

The highest-leverage decision an assessor makes. Everything downstream inherits it: the evidence you request, the interviews you schedule, the requirements you evaluate, the score you compute, and the findings you can defend. It is also where the pressure lives — scope is the one part of an assessment an organization has a direct financial interest in shrinking.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session you will be able to:

  • CategorizeCategorize every asset in an environment into the five CMMC Level 2 asset categories and justify each against the defining test.
  • ApplyApply the two conditional categories — Contractor Risk Managed Assets and Specialized Assets — including the conditions each depends on.
  • StateState the assessment boundary and document what places each asset inside or outside it.
  • AnalyseAnalyse External Service Provider relationships under 32 CFR §170.19, distinguish an ESP from a Cloud Service Provider, and say when a Customer Responsibility Matrix is required and when FedRAMP Moderate or documented equivalency is triggered.
  • TraceTrace flowdown obligations to subcontractors and suppliers.

Where this sits in the Meridian story

Day 2 ended with a determination that Meridian was not ready and that the C3PAO could not proceed. That was the correct outcome and it stands.

Today and tomorrow are not that assessment. They are the work that makes you capable of conducting one: scoping an environment, planning what evidence would prove what, and rendering determinations against it. 2

The five categories

Each category answers one question: what does this asset do with CUI? Everything else follows.

CategoryThe defining testWhat the contractor must doHow it is assessed
CUI AssetsProcesses, stores, or transmits CUIAsset inventory; SSPAgainst all Level 2 security requirements
Security Protection AssetsProvides security functions or capabilities to the assessment scope, whether or not it touches CUIInventory; SSP; network diagramAgainst the requirements relevant to the security function provided
Contractor Risk Managed AssetsCan, but is not intended to, process, store, or transmit CUI because of security policy, procedures, and practices in placeInventory; SSP; network diagramSSP review primarily; further checks if documentation raises concerns
Specialized AssetsCan process, store, or transmit CUI but is unable to be fully securedInventory; SSP; network diagram; demonstrate management through risk-based security policiesSSP review only; not assessed against the other requirements
Out-of-Scope AssetsCannot process, store, or transmit CUI, and provides no security protection for CUI AssetsBe prepared to justify the inabilityNot assessed
Specialized Assets are a closed list

Internet of Things and Industrial Internet of Things devices, Operational Technology, Government Furnished Equipment, Restricted Information Systems, and Test Equipment. That is the list. An asset that is inconvenient to secure, old, or owned by somebody else does not become a Specialized Asset because it would be helpful if it were.

The condition that decides most of this session

Read the Contractor Risk Managed test again: can, but is not intended to, handle CUI — because of security policy, procedures, and practices in place. The relief depends on those existing.

Now read the Specialized Asset obligation: documented in the inventory and the SSP, shown on the network diagram, and demonstrably managed through risk-based security policies.

Both categories are earned, not claimed. And note how little it takes to lose them. Meridian has an SSP, ten policies and an accurate network diagram — it fails the condition on one artifact, the asset inventory, which does not exist. One missing document closes both categories to every asset in the environment.

That is a more instructive failure than a company with nothing, because it is the one you will actually meet: organisations that have done most of the work and cannot claim the relief because of the piece they skipped.

3

External Service Providers and Cloud Service Providers

Most organizations of any size have handed part of their environment to somebody else. The regulation treats that in two different ways depending on what the provider is.

External Service Provider (non-cloud)Cloud Service Provider
What triggers the ruleThe ESP processes, stores, or transmits CUI, or handles Security Protection DataThe CSP processes, stores, or transmits CUI
What is requiredThe relationship, services, and responsibility matrix documented in the SSP. A Customer Responsibility Matrix is required.FedRAMP Moderate authorization, or documented equivalency, under DFARS 252.204-7012
Effect on the assessmentThe ESP is evaluated inside the OSC's assessment. Its personnel are expected to participate.The authorization or equivalency is evidence the OSC must produce. The assessment does not evaluate the cloud service itself.
Two answers, not one

Categorizing a provider and stating its obligation are separate steps, and teams routinely collapse them and answer neither properly. Microsoft 365 is a CUI Asset by category and a CSP question by obligation. Both belong in your matrix.

4

The contested calls

Five categorizations at Meridian attract argument every time. Work each against the defining test, not against what the asset is for.

AssetThe tempting answerWhy the test says otherwise
The CORP-LAN workstations Contractor Risk Managed — no CUI is stored on them by design The relief requires the asset to be documented in the asset inventory, the SSP, and the network diagram. Meridian has an SSP and a network diagram and no asset inventory at all. One missing artifact closes the category. They route freely to SYS-003 and SYS-004 with domain credentials. CUI Assets.
The Xerox AltaLink C8045 IoT, therefore Specialized It produces hard-copy CUI from the PLM and the file server, so it processes CUI. The Specialized argument requires the device to be unable to be fully secured — an enterprise MFP with a management interface generally is securable, which defeats it. Ask separately what it retains on internal storage. CUI Asset.
The domain controllers Out of scope — they hold no CUI They provide authentication, group policy and access control to the entire scope, including Huntsville over the tunnel. Security Protection Assets.
The Cisco Catalyst 2960-X Just plumbing It carries CORP-LAN, ENG-LAN and MGMT-LAN with no VLAN enforcement, which is the single fact that makes almost nothing scopable out. Security Protection Asset — and the most consequential asset in the environment.
The Huntsville utility closet equipment Someone else's building, someone else's problem The Cisco RV345 terminates the IPsec tunnel carrying CUI between the sites. That Meridian does not control the building access system is a serious physical finding, not a reason to scope the device out. Security Protection Asset.

The second site

Huntsville has six engineering workstations, a Cisco RV345, and a UPS, on an always-on IPsec tunnel back to the Dayton domain controllers. The workstations authenticate against Dayton AD and reach the same CUI stores. There is no local IT staff, the site is a commercial office lease, and Meridian does not control the building access system — the switch and UPS sit in an unlocked utility closet accessible to building maintenance.

Huntsville is inside the boundary and the six workstations are CUI Assets on the same reasoning as the Dayton fourteen. The physical exposure is a serious PE finding that does not change the category. A team that also flags “we cannot tell whether the tunnel is the only path between the sites, or whether the Huntsville machines store CUI locally” as open items has done the better piece of work.

5

Justifications that work

Every categorization needs a basis that references the defining test rather than restating what the asset is.

A justification that worksA justification that does not
“Stores CUI: the /CUI contract folder resides on this volume.”“File server.”
“Provides the access control boundary for the assessment scope.”“It is a firewall, so it is a security thing.”
“Can reach the PLM through a browser on the same flat subnet; no policy prevents it.”“Production floor device, not used for CUI.”
“Cannot reach any CUI asset; on a physically separate circuit with no route to the LAN.”“Nobody uses it for CUI.”
Build the inventory before you categorize

An inventory built while categorizing becomes an argument. Assets that would be inconvenient to scope in quietly fail to get listed, and nobody notices, because the list looks complete. List every asset first, recording what it can reach — then categorize. It is also how you find the assets the organization forgot to mention.

6

Where scope errors go

A scoping error does not announce itself. It surfaces on Day 4 as a requirement nobody evaluated, on Day 5 as a finding that cannot be supported, and in practice as a certificate that covers less than the organization believes it does.

The asymmetry matters

Scoping an asset in that did not need to be in costs the organization time. Scoping an asset out that should have been in produces an assessment result that is wrong. Those are not equivalent errors, and when you are genuinely uncertain, the direction to err is not the comfortable one.

The boundary at Meridian, stated correctly

Everything at both sites is inside the boundary except Whitmore & Associates and FastenerTech. Two specific absences put it there: no enforced segmentation, so every CORP-LAN machine reaches the CUI stores on ENG-LAN; and no asset inventory, which closes both conditional categories to the entire environment at once.

Note what makes this different from a company with nothing. Meridian has an SSP, ten policies, and a network diagram. It still cannot claim the conditional treatments, because the one artifact those treatments depend on is the one it does not have. Saying that precisely — rather than “they have no documentation” — is the difference between a Competent and an Assessor-Ready boundary statement.

Meridian — your turn

Meridian · Day 3 Morning · Deliverable D4

Categorize every asset and draw the boundary

The Scope & Asset Matrix tool loads the Meridian environment asset by asset. For each one you record what it can reach, assign a category, and write a one-line basis referencing the defining test. The tool checks each call and tells you why — including on the five that are genuinely contested.

Then five parts, in order, because the boundary has to follow from the matrix rather than the other way round:

  • The matrix — every asset, categorized, justified.
  • The boundary — named systems in and out, and what places each there.
  • ESP and CSP analysis — each provider classified, the resulting obligation named, and whether a Customer Responsibility Matrix is required and exists.
  • Flowdown — which suppliers must receive DFARS 252.204-7012, and on what basis.
  • Open items — what you could not determine, and what evidence would settle it. There are at least two.

Where a category is unavailable to Meridian because a condition is unmet, say so explicitly and name the condition. And no remediation anywhere: “this asset is in scope because there is no segmentation” is assessment; “they should segment the network” is not yours to write.
