Transfer Test — Acuitas Machining

Thirty minutes on a company you have never seen: a supplier inside Meridian’s own supply chain. Fourteen people, no server, no domain, and three CNC controllers that retain CUI for months.

30 minutesIndividualNo notesKey locked until the clock stops
Timed · individual · cold 30:00

Before you start

Thirty minutes. Individually. No notes, no Meridian material, no internet. You may use the CMMC Assessment Scope guidance for Level 2 and 32 CFR §170.19 if you have them in hard copy.

You will receive a profile of a company in Meridian’s supply chain and produce a scope determination. Budget roughly eight minutes to read and twenty to write.

What is being measured

Not recall. Whether the method you have used on one company for four days works on a company you have never seen — which is the only thing that would make you useful to the next OSC that hires you.

A confident wrong categorization scores worse than a correctly hedged one. If a category is genuinely arguable, say what makes it arguable and what would settle it. That is the assessor’s answer, and it is the answer this test is looking for.

This is not a trick. Everything you need is in the profile, and the reasoning is the reasoning you have used since Lesson 4. The only new thing is the company.

Acuitas Machining Inc.

LocationSpringfield, Ohio — about 25 miles from Meridian’s Dayton plant
BusinessPrecision CNC machining and inspection — a job shop taking overflow and short-run work
Employees14 — the owner, an office manager, a shop lead, eight machinists, two part-time CNC programmers, plus an external bookkeeper
Revenue~$3.4M annually
DoD-related work~$800K, all of it as a sub-tier supplier to Meridian Precision Defense Systems
Other workAutomotive tooling, agricultural equipment parts, and two local fabricators. No government relationship.
FacilityOne leased building: a shop floor, a small office, and an inspection room.

What Acuitas receives from Meridian

Meridian subcontracts machining overflow on the actuator housing line. To do that work, Acuitas receives:

  • Machining drawings for the actuator housing assemblies, extracted from the technical data package AMCOM furnished to Meridian.
  • Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing data, material specifications, and inspection criteria.
  • Lot quantities and delivery dates for each release.

Files arrive by a shared SharePoint link from Meridian’s GCC High tenant; Acuitas saves them down to its own commercial cloud drive. There is no formal agreement between the two companies beyond purchase orders, and the purchase orders reference “MPDS standard terms” which incorporate DFARS 252.204-7012 by reference. Nobody at Acuitas has read them. The owner’s position, stated plainly during the walkthrough, is that “we are a job shop. We machine to a print. CMMC is Meridian’s problem.”

The environment

There is no server, no domain, and no IT staff. There is no system security plan, no asset inventory, no network diagram, no written security policy of any kind, and no record of any security decision ever having been made. What exists:

Answer sheet

Question 1 — Level and authority (about 4 minutes)

What CMMC level applies to Acuitas, and what makes it apply? Answer the owner’s objection directly, in no more than four sentences, and name the instrument you are relying on.

Question 3 — The consequential unknown (about 4 minutes)

Name the single fact you would most want resolved before finalizing this scope, say why it matters, and say what evidence would settle it.

Question 2 — asset categorization, about 14 minutes — is below, one asset at a time.

The determination key is locked until the clock stops or you unlock it deliberately. Answer first.

Question 1 — the key

Expected answer: CMMC Level 2

Acuitas receives machining drawings and tolerancing data extracted from the technical data package AMCOM furnished to Meridian. That is Controlled Technical Information — CUI — and it reaches Acuitas through a subcontract that incorporates DFARS 252.204-7012, which flows down under paragraph (m) to subcontractors whose performance will involve covered defense information. The obligation follows the information, not the tier.

The owner’s objection is answered directly: the clause is in his subcontract, flowdown is the mechanism by which it got there, Meridian was required to put it there, and whether he has read it is not relevant to whether it binds him.

Marking notes. Full credit requires naming the flowdown mechanism, not merely asserting Level 2 — “they handle CUI so it is Level 2” is the conclusion without the authority. Either 252.204-7012(m) or DFARS 252.204-7021 flowdown is acceptable as the cited instrument provided the reasoning is sound; naming both and distinguishing them earns more. Do not accept “Level 1, because they are a subcontractor” — the single most serious error available on this test, and the owner’s own error. Noting that Acuitas’s commercial work carries no federal obligation at all, and that only the Meridian work triggers this, is worth extra credit.

1

Commercial cloud tenant and shared drive

A commercial Microsoft 365 Business Standard subscription — email and OneDrive. Not a government or high-assurance tenant. MFA is enabled on the owner’s account only. One folder structure: Meridian drawings land in a folder named “MPDS jobs,” commercial customers’ work sits in sibling folders, and all fourteen staff accounts can read every folder.

Model answer

CUI Asset. It stores and transmits CUI, so it is a CUI Asset. Then the separate obligation: because it is an external cloud service processing CUI, Acuitas must produce FedRAMP Moderate authorisation or documented equivalency under DFARS 252.204-7012(b)(2)(ii)(D) — and a commercial Business Standard tenant does not carry one.

Note the contrast with Meridian. Meridian’s GCC High tenant passes the authorisation and fails on configuration. Acuitas fails at the authorisation itself. Same rule, two entirely different findings — and only the second one can be fixed by configuration. Credit students who separate the categorization from the authorisation question rather than collapsing them.

2

Office PCs (2)

Owner and office manager. Used for email, quoting, scheduling, and opening drawings.

Model answer

CUI Asset. They open and store CUI drawings. Straightforward; nearly everyone gets this.

3

Shop floor PC

Opens drawings and generates the tool paths sent to the machines. Shared login used by whoever is on shift; the password is taped inside the cabinet door.

Model answer

CUI Asset. Processes CUI. The shared login is a finding, not a categorization question — watch for students who confuse a control failure with a scoping decision.

4

Three CNC machining centres

Each has an embedded Windows-based controller. Tool-path and drawing-derived files are transferred by USB and retained on the controller’s internal storage until an operator clears them. The shop lead clears them “when they get full — maybe twice a year.”

Model answer

Specialized Asset — but the treatment is unavailable. The pivot of the whole test. These are Operational Technology, they process and retain CUI, and OT is a listed Specialized Asset type. But the Specialized treatment is conditional: the contractor must document the asset in the inventory, the SSP and the network diagram, and demonstrate that it is managed using risk-based security policies. Acuitas has none of those artifacts and no policy of any kind. The category is therefore unavailable in practice, and the asset cannot simply be set aside.

Three answers appear and they separate the cohort cleanly. Weakest: out of scope, “they are machine tools” — the asset has not been read as an information system and the months-long retention has been missed. Middle: full CUI Assets requiring all 110 requirements — thorough, but unaware the Specialized category exists. Strongest: Specialized by type, treatment unavailable by condition, so they stay in the assessment. That is the difference between having read the scoping guide and being able to use it.

5

CMM inspection station

A coordinate measuring machine with an attached PC. Inspection programs are written from the Meridian drawings and stored on the PC; measurement reports go back to Meridian with the parts.

Model answer

CUI Asset. It holds programs derived from CUI drawings and generates inspection data referencing them. Students who scope the machine tools carefully and then forget the metrology station have found the obvious OT and missed the quieter one.

6

Wireless network

One WPA2 network, one passphrase, unchanged since installation. Shared by staff, the two part-time programmers, and visiting customers. No guest network, no segmentation.

Model answer

Security Protection Asset. It provides the access control boundary such as it is, and it is the reason nothing at Acuitas can be scoped out. “In-scope infrastructure” with sound reasoning is acceptable.

7

Bookkeeper (external, own laptop)

Part-time, works from home on her own equipment. Holds an account on the cloud tenant with access to the full shared drive, because restricting it was never set up.

Model answer

External Service Provider — in scope. She holds an account with access to CUI and works from her own equipment. An ESP that handles CUI is assessed within the OSC assessment and requires a Customer Responsibility Matrix. None exists. Watch for students who dismiss her as “just accounting.”

8

IT contractor (tenant administrator)

A local one-person firm that set up the tenant and the wireless network. Holds administrator rights on the cloud tenant. Called when something breaks; no contract beyond an hourly rate sheet.

Model answer

External Service Provider — Security Protection. Holds tenant administrator rights, so he handles Security Protection Data. Same treatment: assessed inside the assessment, CRM required — and there is no contract beyond a rate sheet. Note the structural echo: Meridian has exactly this problem with ClearPath, one tier up. The pattern repeats down the supply chain, and nobody at either company has looked.

Question 3 — the key

No single required answer — three are strong

  • What the CNC controllers retain, and for how long. The best answer. It decides whether the controllers are information systems holding CUI or transient devices, and it is the difference between the hardest categorization on the sheet and an easy one. Settled by examining the controller storage directly — an inspection, not an interview.
  • Whether the cloud tenant can meet FedRAMP Moderate or documented equivalency. It determines whether the environment can be brought into conformity at all, or whether Acuitas must move tenants before an assessment is meaningful. Settled by the provider’s authorization record and the subscription terms.
  • Whether any risk-based security policy exists in any form. The most consequential in breadth: it is the condition on both the Specialized Asset and Contractor Risk Managed treatments. If nothing exists, two categories collapse into CUI Assets at once. Settled by asking for the document and accepting only a document.

Weak answers. “Whether they will fix the wifi” — a remediation question, not an assessment one, and the consulting reflex arriving under time pressure. “How much CUI there is” — volume does not change categorization. Anything the profile already answers — re-reading is part of the thirty minutes.

What should be scoped out

Nothing — and saying so explicitly is the correct answer

Out-of-Scope Assets require the contractor to be prepared to justify the asset’s inability to process, store, or transmit CUI. On a single flat wireless network with a shared drive every account can read, no such justification exists.

A student who scopes nothing out, and says explicitly that the absence of segmentation is what makes that true, has produced the correct answer and should be told so.

Where you stand

LevelWhat it looks like
Not YetLevel 1, or Level 2 with no authority named. Assets categorized by intuition. Printer treated as out of scope.
DevelopingLevel 2 correctly identified with the flowdown named. Most assets categorized plausibly. Printer treated as a full CUI Asset, or as Specialized without noticing the condition. ESPs missed or dismissed.
CompetentLevel 2 with the flowdown mechanism cited and the owner’s objection answered. All eight assets categorized with sound one-line bases. Both ESPs identified. Printer identified as a Specialized Asset. A defensible consequential unknown.
Assessor-ReadyAll of the above, plus the conditional nature of the Specialized and Contractor Risk Managed treatments recognised explicitly — the categories exist, and Acuitas cannot claim them. States what would have to be true for the answer to change.

This is diagnostic, not graded, and it does not affect completion. It exists to tell you something you cannot find out any other way: whether the week gave you a method or a familiarity. Both feel identical from the inside.
