Practice Activities: Practice It, Then Teach It

Nine self-checking activities, one for each of Lessons 0 to 8. Work them yourself as a CCP candidate: sort, rank, calculate, and judge exam-style cases on the fictional Meridian company. Then use each one as a model for an activity in a course you teach.

Activities: 9Each: 30 to 45 minDomains: All six CCPFormat: Self-checkingReports: Print or PDF

How practice works

Practice it yourself, then use it as a model in your course

Stations

Each activity has two or three scored stations and a reflection: sorts, rankings, calculations, and exam-style cases.

Self-checking

Every answer is checked on demand, with the reasoning for the right answer and for each wrong one. Retry any station as often as you like.

Concept mastery

A meter tracks each concept as you work, with a percentage and a mark, and the report suggests what to review next.

Teach with it

Each reflection ends with a faculty prompt: how you would adapt the activity for your own students.

For faculty

Using these activities in your own course

  • Practice first: complete each activity yourself before you assign it. Note which distractor tempted you; your students will likely pick the same one.
  • As a model: each activity pairs one concept with one kind of judgment (sort, rank, calculate, or decide a case). Keep that pattern and swap in examples from your own field or region, labeled as fictional.
  • As submissions: students can print the summary report to PDF and upload it. Reports show scores by station, attempts, missed items with the reasoning, reflections, and time spent.
  • In class: run one station as a think-pair-share, then let pairs check answers and argue over the rationale before you discuss it.
  • Keep it current: regulatory items will age. Recheck the Phase 2 status, the clause numbers, and the POA&M rules against primary sources each term.
