Domain Determination Worksheet

Eight requirements across six families, decomposed into their assessment objectives with the Meridian evidence attached. Determine each objective, cite the evidence, and argue the two disputed mappings.

8 requirementsObjective level2 mapping disputesPrints as D6

Three determinations

DeterminationWhat it meansWhat it requires of you
METAll applicable objectives satisfied by final-form evidence. An enduring exception with documented mitigation, or a temporary deficiency covered by an operational plan of action, can still be MET.Cite evidence for each objective.
NOT METOne or more objectives is not satisfied.Name the objective and what is missing.
NOT APPLICABLEThe requirement does not apply to the scope.Justify it. N/A scores as MET, so it will be challenged.

Work at the objective level

A worksheet that determines at the requirement level has not done the work. Every objective is listed for you below. Read each one, decide whether the evidence reaches it, and let the requirement determination follow.

There is no partial credit. One objective unmet makes the whole requirement NOT MET.

On the objective text below

The assessment objectives listed for each requirement are the decompositions in NIST SP 800-171A, reproduced here so you can work without switching documents. They are a study aid, not the authority. In a real assessment you work from the CMMC Assessment Guide for Level 2 and NIST SP 800-171A directly, and you verify the objective text and count against the current published version before you rely on it.

Citation standard: “Interview with T. Nakamura, 14 March, on privileged account inventory; access control matrix v1.2 §3” is a citation. “Discussion” is not. And no remediation anywhere — state what is missing, not what to do about it.

1

AC.L2-3.1.1

AC.L2-3.1.1 — Limit system access to authorized users, processes acting on behalf of authorized users, and devices

Assessment objectives

  1. Authorized users are identified
  2. Processes acting on behalf of authorized users are identified
  3. Devices authorized to connect are identified
  4. System access is limited to authorized users
  5. System access is limited to processes acting on behalf of authorized users
  6. System access is limited to authorized devices
Evidence available

Six VPN accounts belong to employees who separated between three and fourteen months ago; HR notifies IT by email with no checklist and VPN deprovisioning is not on it. A shared service account holds Global Admin in the GCC High tenant with no individual owner. ClearPath technicians reach domain admin over the VPN. No asset inventory exists, so no register of authorized devices exists either.

Model answer

NOT MET. No objective survives. Authorized users are not identified in any maintained artifact; devices authorized to connect are not identified at all, because there is no inventory; and access is demonstrably not limited to authorized users, which the six live accounts prove. A finding here names all six objectives, not “access control is weak.”

AC.L2-3.1.5

AC.L2-3.1.5 — Employ the principle of least privilege, including for specific security functions and privileged accounts

Assessment objectives

  1. Privileged accounts are identified
  2. Access to privileged accounts is authorized per least privilege
  3. Security functions are identified
  4. Access to security functions is authorized per least privilege
Evidence available

ClearPath holds standing domain administrator rights under a month-to-month agreement with no cybersecurity terms and no background-check verification. Policy POL-001 §4.3 requires quarterly review of privileged accounts; no review record exists. The shared mpds-admin@ account holds Global Admin with no individual owner.

Model answer

NOT MET. Privileged accounts are not identified in any maintained register; access to them is not authorized against a least-privilege standard; security functions are not documented; and a standing external domain admin defeats the principle outright. Note this is a 3-point requirement, not 5 — a frequent scoring error. Note also that the policy requires exactly the review that is missing, which strengthens the finding rather than mitigating it.

IA.L2-3.5.3

IA.L2-3.5.3 — Use multifactor authentication for local and network access to privileged accounts and for network access to non-privileged accounts

Assessment objectives

  1. Privileged accounts are identified
  2. MFA is implemented for local access to privileged accounts
  3. MFA is implemented for network access to privileged accounts
  4. Non-privileged accounts are identified
  5. MFA is implemented for network access to non-privileged accounts
Evidence available

Conditional Access enforces MFA across the GCC High tenant for all 163 accounts, all cloud apps, any location — with the shared mpds-admin@ Global Admin service account excluded. The FortiGate SSL VPN, which is the network access path for roughly 22 users including ClearPath, authenticates against Active Directory with username and password alone. AWS GovCloud root has MFA enabled.

Model answer

NOT MET. The tenant evidence is excellent and covers one layer. Network access to privileged accounts fails at the VPN; network access to non-privileged accounts fails at the VPN; and the two “accounts are identified” objectives fail because no privileged-account register is maintained. The Conditional Access exclusion is a separate and serious observation: the most privileged identity in the tenant is exempt from the policy that covers everyone else.

AU.L2-3.3.1

AU.L2-3.3.1 — Create and retain system audit logs and records sufficient to enable monitoring, analysis, investigation, and reporting

Assessment objectives

  1. Audit logs needed to enable monitoring, analysis, investigation, and reporting of unlawful or unauthorized system activity are specified
  2. The content of audit records needed to support monitoring, analysis, investigation, and reporting is defined
  3. Audit records are created (generated)
  4. Audit records, once created, contain the defined content
  5. Retention requirements for audit records are defined
  6. Audit records are retained as defined
Evidence available

SYS-003 and SYS-004 generate logs. Retention is 30 days, which Nakamura describes in interview as the platform default rather than a decision. No formal review process exists and no SIEM is deployed. Snort runs on MGMT-LAN; nobody is identified as reading its output.

Model answer

NOT MET. Audit records are created, so that objective may be satisfied. The logs needed have never been specified; the content of audit records has never been defined, so the objective requiring records to contain the defined content cannot be reached at all; and a retention period inherited as a default has not been determined against the standard the requirement sets, which is sufficiency to support investigation. One objective possibly MET does not carry the requirement.

5

CA.L2-3.12.4

CA.L2-3.12.4 — Develop, document, and periodically update a system security plan that describes system boundaries, environments of operation, security requirement implementation, and relationships with other systems

Assessment objectives

  1. A system security plan is developed
  2. The system boundary is described and documented
  3. The system environment of operation is described and documented
  4. Security requirements identified and approved by the designated authority as non-applicable are identified
  5. The method of security requirement implementation is described and documented
  6. The relationship with or connection to other systems is described and documented
  7. The frequency to update the system security plan is defined
  8. The system security plan is updated with the defined frequency
Evidence available

SSP POL-008 version 2.1, dated October 2023. Meridian’s own document register marks it “needs update.” The company has a network diagram. The SSP does not describe the ClearPath relationship, the Acuitas or AeroComp data flows, or the manual upload path from SYS-003 into AWS GovCloud. No update frequency is stated anywhere in it.

Model answer

NOT MET. This is the requirement where having documents makes the assessment harder, not easier. A plan exists and a boundary is described, so two objectives may be satisfied — and Meridian will argue from there. But relationships with other systems are not described: three CUI-handling external parties and an inter-cloud data flow are absent. No update frequency is defined, so the last two objectives cannot be reached, and the company’s own register concedes the plan is out of date.

Consequence: CA.L2-3.12.4 is worth one point and is one of the six that may never be placed on a POA&M. A NOT MET here blocks any CMMC status regardless of score.

IR.L2-3.6.1

IR.L2-3.6.1 — Establish an operational incident-handling capability that includes preparation, detection, analysis, containment, recovery, and user response activities

Assessment objectives

  1. An operational incident-handling capability is established
  2. Preparation is addressed
  3. Detection is addressed
  4. Analysis is addressed
  5. Containment is addressed
  6. Recovery is addressed
  7. User response activities are addressed
Evidence available

Incident Response Plan POL-002 v1.0, dated March 2022, marked “stale — 3+ yrs” in the company’s own register. Never exercised. The contact roster names a project manager who left in early 2024. The plan contains no DFARS 72-hour reporting language. Two security events in Oct 2023 and Feb 2024 were handled by chat and email; neither was assessed against the clause; zero reports have been filed in 24 months.

Model answer

NOT MET. A documented plan is not an operational capability. The plan has never been tested, names a responder who does not work here, and omits the reporting obligation entirely — so user response activities and analysis are not addressed in any operative sense. The two unassessed events are the proof: when the capability was needed twice, it did not function.

MA.L2-3.7.5

MA.L2-3.7.5 — Require multifactor authentication to establish nonlocal maintenance sessions via external network connections, and terminate such connections when nonlocal maintenance is complete

Assessment objectives

  1. MFA is required to establish nonlocal maintenance sessions via external network connections
  2. Nonlocal maintenance sessions are terminated when maintenance is complete
Evidence available

ClearPath performs remote administration over the FortiGate SSL VPN, which has no MFA. The engagement is month-to-month with no security terms. There is no session recording and no access logging configured for the MSP’s access.

Model answer

NOT MET. Both objectives fail, and cleanly. MFA is not required to establish the session — that is the same VPN gap as IA.L2-3.5.3, appearing here in its maintenance aspect. And with no session logging there is no evidence whatever that sessions terminate on completion; standing access is by design the opposite of a session that ends. Note this requirement is where the MSP relationship becomes a technical finding rather than a contractual one.

PE.L2-3.10.1

PE.L2-3.10.1 — Limit physical access to organizational systems, equipment, and the respective operating environments to authorized individuals

Assessment objectives

  1. Authorized individuals allowed physical access are identified
  2. Physical access to systems is limited to authorized individuals
  3. Physical access to equipment is limited to authorized individuals
  4. Physical access to operating environments is limited to authorized individuals
Evidence available

Dayton: HID card readers, Server Room 104 on card plus PIN restricted to three named people, MSP escorted under an informal, undocumented escort policy. The engineering area has no separate access control — any corporate badge holder can reach the CAD/CAM workstations. Huntsville: commercial office lease, Meridian does not control the building access system, and the network switch and UPS sit in an unlocked utility closet accessible to building maintenance.

Model answer

NOT MET. Dayton is largely creditable and Huntsville is not, and the requirement applies across the assessment scope. Authorized individuals for the Huntsville equipment are not identified and cannot be — Meridian does not control who holds building access. Physical access to equipment carrying CUI between the sites is not limited to authorized individuals by any mechanism the company operates.

Watch for teams that assess only the headquarters. A single site meeting a requirement does not carry an environment that spans two.

Mapping dispute — GAP-04 — “No asset inventory; unpatched engineering workstations”

GAP-04 — “No asset inventory; unpatched engineering workstations”, which the company maps to 3.4.2, described in its own documentation as “establish and maintain a component inventory”

Do you accept the company’s mapping? If not, give yours.

Model answer

CM.L2-3.4.1 and 3.4.2. The company’s description of 3.4.2 is wrong. In Rev 2, 3.4.1 is the requirement that covers baseline configurations and inventories; 3.4.2 is establishing and enforcing security configuration settings. The gap as written is really two gaps against two requirements.

This dispute does not move the score — both are 5-point requirements — which is exactly why it is worth catching. It changes what evidence closes it: an inventory closes 3.4.1 and does nothing for 3.4.2, and a patch report closes neither on its own.

10

Mapping dispute — GAP-05 — “No formal POA&M; informal spreadsheet

GAP-05 — “No formal POA&M; informal spreadsheet, not AO-approved”, which the company maps to 3.12.4

Do you accept the company’s mapping? If not, give yours.

Model answer

CA.L2-3.12.2. 3.12.4 is the System Security Plan requirement. A deficient plan of action is CA.L2-3.12.2, which concerns developing and implementing plans of action to correct deficiencies.

The consequence is not academic. CA.L2-3.12.4 is one of the six requirements that may never be placed on a POA&M; 3.12.2 is not. Which requirement this gap belongs to helps decide whether Meridian is blocked from any CMMC status outright.

Note the second-order point: Meridian’s SSP is version 2.1 from October 2023 and its own register marks it “needs update.” So 3.12.4 is arguably NOT MET on its own merits — the requirement includes periodic update — and the block may hold either way. An assessor who reaches that conclusion by two independent routes has done the work.

11

Mapping dispute — GAP-08 — “No segmentation between CORP-LAN and ENG-LAN”

GAP-08 — “No segmentation between CORP-LAN and ENG-LAN”, which the company maps to 3.13.3

Do you accept the company’s mapping? If not, give yours.

Model answer

SC.L2-3.13.1. 3.13.3 is separate user functionality from system management functionality — which is arguably engaged by the policy-only MGMT-LAN separation, but is not what the finding describes. What is described is a failure to monitor, control and protect communications at a key internal boundary: SC.L2-3.13.1, worth 5 points rather than 1. The correction raises deductions by 4.

11

Consistency check

Your determinations must be consistent with the scope you drew in Lesson 4 and the evidence you triaged in Lesson 5. Where a determination here would change a call you made earlier, say so.

Model answer

Two places where the Day 5 evidence package should move an earlier call:

  • The SSP now exists, so the Lesson 4 statement that both conditional asset categories are closed to Meridian needs re-testing — and it survives, because the SSP's boundary section names no systems and there is still no network diagram and no written policy. Say that explicitly rather than leaving the earlier reasoning to stand unexamined.
  • MFA was added to the SSL VPN four months ago. That does not change the IA.L2-3.5.3 determination, because domain controller RDP is still single-factor — but it does change the scoring analysis you will do next, and a worksheet that ignores it will produce the wrong deduction.

Noting where a determination here would change a call made earlier is an Assessor-Ready behaviour. It is also how you avoid a findings report that contradicts its own scope statement.
