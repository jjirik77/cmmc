Teaching CMMC — A Faculty and Facilitator Guide

You have worked the material. This is how to deploy it: the 8-week graded version it was derived from, the facilitation decisions that make or break each session, the answer keys and what to do with them, the instruments that must be withheld until issued, and the maintenance obligation that comes with teaching a subject in active regulatory flux.

A

Learning objectives

This guide is for the person delivering the workshop. By the end of it you will be able to:

  • ChooseChoose between the 5-day workshop, the 8-week graded course, and the hybrid, on the basis of cohort and contact hours.
  • FacilitateFacilitate each session's highest-risk moments, including the ones where the correct answer is uncomfortable.
  • AssessAssess against the competency rubric rather than a grade, and know what Competent versus Assessor-Ready looks like.
  • ProtectProtect the instruments that must be issued cold, and know why each one is withheld.
  • MaintainMaintain the material against a regulatory position that is actively moving.

The facilitation decisions that matter

SessionThe momentHow to run it
L1Students raise whatever is currently in the trade press about CMMC changing Answer it on day one using the instrument test, not the news: has a class deviation, a FAR/DFARS rule change, or an amendment to Part 170 issued? If not, the framework is what the published text says. Then redirect. Do not let it recur — and note that you have just modelled the L9 skill in thirty seconds.
L2The CUI audit debrief Have the NARA Registry open and projected. Look items up live rather than reading answers off a key — modelling the lookup is most of the lesson. Item 2 is worth half the session; push students through the temptation to keep “CTI” because it is nearly right, let them fail to find a fitting category, and land on the contract and the contracting officer. Students uncomfortable ending on an open item are learning the correct discomfort.
L3Ethics scenario adjudication One of the eight scenarios is not addressed directly by the CoPC at all. Finding that out is the exercise. An honest “the document does not cover this, so here is what governs” is a stronger answer than a citation that does not say what the student claims.
L4The conditional categories Teach the test before the name. Do the two conditional categories last and slowly: read the condition aloud, then ask what Meridian would show you. The silence is the lesson.
L5Artifact 2, the Conditional Access export The artifact that separates Competent from Assessor-Ready. Watch for students who accept it outright because it is real technical evidence, and students who reject it outright because Meridian fails the requirement. Both miss the point: the artifact is excellent and the requirement is still NOT MET.
L6Cross-presentation Require teams to lead with their least comfortable determination. Teams instinctively present their best work, which wastes the exercise — nobody learns from a determination everyone agrees with.
L7The 3.5.3 deduction Poll the room before revealing. Expect a substantial minority at 3. Work the conjunction on screen: remote and privileged. The spread is the lesson.
L8Ellison's request Play it once per team, late, when they are tired and want to be helpful. A team that answers has failed the exercise regardless of their determinations — and that failure teaches more than the finding would have.
L9The transfer test debrief Ten minutes, immediately, while the reasoning is live. Poll on the printer controller before revealing anything — students remember their own wrong answer better than your right one. Then ask who scoped something out and make them defend it. On that environment nobody should have.
3

What must be withheld

Three cold instruments

The Day 5 evidence package. Issued at the in-brief. A team that has read the nine documents in advance has done a different exercise, and the time pressure that makes the mock assessment realistic disappears.

The interview role cards. Role-players only, one card each, never shown to assessment teams. Every card contains at least one fact that contradicts something in the evidence package, and those contradictions are the assessable content.

The Acuitas transfer test. Do not release the profile in advance, do not preview the company, and do not answer questions about it once the clock starts.

How to play the interview cards

The rule that makes it work: answer the question you were asked, and nothing more. Each card has material that is volunteered freely and material that emerges only if a student asks a question that reaches it — a real question, not a yes-or-no invitation to agree.

If a student asks “you review the logs regularly, correct?”, say yes. That is a true answer to a bad question, and the student learning why it was a bad question is the point of the exercise. If a team asks only closed questions, let the interview end early and uselessly. The silence afterwards teaches more than a rescue would.

Hold the contradictions. If a team leaves without finding any of them, that is the finding — and it is the one to open the debrief with.

Assessing competency rather than grading

Four levels, applied to every deliverable. Competent is the completion standard; Assessor-Ready describes work that would survive professional challenge.

LevelWhat it looks like across the deliverables
Not YetAdopts the contractor's own claims wholesale. Categorizes by intuition or by what an asset is for. Determines at the requirement level. Recommends remediation.
DevelopingHandles the obvious cases and goes quiet on the ambiguous ones. Applies one test but not both. Names a requirement but not the objective. Advice appears once, in passing.
CompetentEvery determination evidenced and traceable. Conditional categories recognised as earned. Both adequacy and sufficiency applied. No advice anywhere; gaps named without fixes.
Assessor-ReadySays what would change each answer. Catches the under-classified item and explains why that direction is more dangerous. Names a gap so precisely that the fix is obvious without ever being stated. Written so that a company that dislikes the result still recognises it as fair.
The single most useful marking habit

Look for the what would change it clause. A determination that cannot describe its own falsifying evidence has not been reasoned — it has been asserted. That one test separates Competent from Developing more reliably than any other, and it is fast to apply.

Maintaining this material

Standing obligation before every run

Re-verify the regulatory position within seven days of any workshop start date. This material deliberately carries no dated policy layer — it teaches 32 CFR Part 170 and the DFARS clause family as published, so that it does not go stale with the news cycle. That design decision only holds if you check the published instruments before you teach.

Apply the same test the workshop teaches: has a class deviation, a FAR or DFARS rule change, or an amendment to Part 170 issued since the last run? If not, nothing here needs changing.

Practical notes

  • Minimum viable cohort is twelve — four teams of three — for the split-case design. Below that, run a single case per session and extend the challenge rounds.
  • Schedule the Day 5 sessions contiguously. The overnight evidence-request bridge only reads as overnight if there is a break; if you must compress, run the request as a 15-minute in-session gap and say so.
  • No purchased software, lab environment, or clearance is required. Every artifact is fictional and unclassified. Every primary source is free and publicly available.
  • Note on dodcio.defense.gov: that host blocks automated tools but opens normally in a browser. Do not report the links as dead.
  • Where a portal module disagrees with this material on who issues the CCP, this material is right and the portal predates December 2025. Use the discrepancy in L0 — it is the cleanest available demonstration of source authority.
