Day 4 · Afternoon · DoD Assessment Methodology · DFARS 252.204-7012

Scoring, SPRS, and the Incident Clock

Determinations become a number. The number is posted to SPRS, read by a contracting officer, and affirmed under signature by a named official. An arithmetic slip here is not a rounding error — it is a false statement in a federal system. Then a device goes missing, and you decide whether a 72-hour clock has started.

Day 4 — PM ~3 hours 110 to −203 Tool: SPRS Calculator

Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to…

  • Calculate a NIST SP 800-171 DoD Assessment score using the 5, 3, and 1 point weighting, from a maximum of 110 to a floor of −203, including the two partial-credit exceptions. Bloom: Apply
  • Distinguish the DoD Assessment score from the CMMC certification determination, and say what each is for. Bloom: Analyze
  • Explain what a posted SPRS score commits a company to, and who personally carries that commitment. Bloom: Understand
  • Rule on whether an incident triggers the DFARS 252.204-7012 72-hour obligation, state when the clock starts, and defend the decision against the strongest counter-argument. Bloom: Apply
  • Identify what a contractor must hold in order to file a report at all. Bloom: Remember

1

Two different things that both look like scoring

Keep these separate from the first minute

A CMMC assessment determination is binary at the objective level. Every objective is MET or NOT APPLICABLE, or the requirement is NOT MET. There is no partial credit.

The DoD Assessment Methodology score is a weighted 110-point scale with deductions of 5, 3, or 1 — and two requirements where partial credit does exist.

These are different instruments serving different purposes, and candidates conflate them every year.

The methodology exists because DoD needed a way to compare the security posture of thousands of contractors at acquisition time, quickly, from self-reported data. It is a strategic risk instrument. A CMMC certification assessment is a conformity determination about one organization at one moment. A company can hold a respectable SPRS score and fail a certification assessment, and the reverse is arithmetically possible too.

2

The weighting, and why it is what it is

Start at 110 and subtract. The weighting reflects what an unimplemented requirement would let an attacker do — not how hard the requirement is to satisfy.

ValueWhat it coversExamples
5 pointsRequirements whose absence renders other controls ineffective or permits significant exploitation or exfiltrationLimiting system access to authorized users; establishing baseline configurations; incident-handling capability; controlling removable media; vulnerability scanning
3 pointsRequirements with a specific and confined security effectLimiting access to CUI on system media; encrypting CUI on mobile devices; least privilege; tracing actions to individual users
1 pointThe remaining derived requirements, with limited or indirect effectPreventing reuse of identifiers for a defined period; developing and updating the System Security Plan
The two partial-credit exceptions — and only these two
RequirementDeduct 3Deduct 5
3.5.3 — Multifactor authenticationMFA implemented for remote and privileged users, but not for the general userMFA not implemented for any users
3.13.11 — FIPS-validated encryptionEncryption is employed but is not FIPS validatedEncryption is not employed

Every other requirement is all or nothing. The temptation to award partial credit elsewhere, because an organization has “mostly” done something, is the most common scoring error there is.

The floor

The maximum is 110. The minimum is −203, which is what you get by implementing nothing: the weighted deductions total far more than 110. A negative score is not a computational error, and contractors do post them.

3

The 3.5.3 trap, worked

Meridian's gap G-01 reads: MFA on Microsoft 365 only; PLM, file server, domain controller RDP, VPN, QuickBooks, and the backup NAS all single-factor. What is the deduction?

A substantial minority answer 3, reasoning that MFA is partly implemented. Read the exception precisely: the 3-point treatment requires MFA for remote and privileged users. Meridian has neither — the SSL VPN is remote access with no MFA, and domain controller RDP is privileged access with no MFA. Microsoft 365 alone earns nothing under the exception.

Deduct 5.

Exam tip

The exception is a conjunction, not a spectrum. “Some MFA exists” is not the test. Ask specifically: is MFA in place for remote users? Is it in place for privileged users? Both yes and the general user missing is a 3. Either one missing is a 5.

GAP-06 is the most instructive line on the sheet

Practices 3.17.1 and 3.17.2 belong to the SR — Supply Chain Risk Management — family in NIST SP 800-171 Rev 3. CMMC Level 2 is anchored to Rev 2, which has fourteen families and no SR family at all.

So there is nothing to deduct. That does not make the gap harmless — an MSP with unaudited domain admin and three CUI-handling suppliers with no flow-down is among the most serious exposures in the company. It is a DFARS 252.204-7012(m) flow-down violation and an ESP treatment failure under 32 CFR §170.19, and its access-control consequences are already scored at 3.1.1, 3.5.3 and 3.7.5.

Score it zero and write it up as a contractual finding. An assessor who deducts against a requirement that does not exist in the applicable revision has produced a number nobody can defend.

The arithmetic

Starting score 110. Total deductions 51. Computed score 59. 59 ÷ 110 = 0.536 — well short of the 0.8 POA&M eligibility threshold. Meridian cannot achieve even Conditional Level 2 status on this gap list.

Two things a strong answer adds

The score is not the only gate. PE.L2-3.10.3 is NOT MET and is one of the six requirements that may never be placed on a POA&M. CA.L2-3.12.4 is arguably NOT MET too, on the SSP’s own “needs update” status. Even above 88, those must be MET outright before any status could issue.

59 is an optimistic ceiling, not a floor. Eight seeded findings expanded across fourteen lines is a gap analysis of selected areas, not an assessment of 110 requirements. Media disposal, physical access at Dayton, and the uncontrolled upload path into AWS GovCloud are three the list never reaches. Any figure between 50 and 59 with the reasoning shown is correct; a bare number is not.

5

What the score commits a company to

Meridian eventually submitted a score of 88 to SPRS. Priya Mehta prepared it from a spreadsheet the consultant provided. She did not check the arithmetic. Marcus Ellison signed it.

Ellison affirmed 88 on the basis that “Priya and the consultant said that was the number.” Ellison did not verify it and would not know how, and does not know what an Affirming Official is personally liable for.

ElementWhat it involves
Where it is postedSPRS, as the assessment of record
Why it matters at awardA condition of award under DFARS 252.204-7019 and -7020; the Contracting Officer verifies it
Who affirmsA senior official of the OSC with authority to bind it — not the IT manager, not the consultant, not the assessor
What it attestsContinued compliance. A present-tense statement, not a memory of the assessment
The exposureAn affirmation that is not true is a false statement in a federal system, with False Claims Act consequences attaching personally to the official who signed

Hold Ellison in mind on Friday when you write the sustainment section. Nobody at Meridian behaved dishonestly. The number is still wrong, and Ellison is still the person who attested to it.

6

The incident clock: INC-003

Wednesday, 09:40

Trevor Nakamura, reconciling VPN accounts against the HR separation list in preparation for the assessment, finds that one of the six stale accounts — jhollis, belonging to a design engineer who left in November — shows successful VPN authentications on four dates over the past six weeks, the most recent eleven days ago. Each session lasted between twenty and ninety minutes.

Known: the account was never disabled. It held membership in the engineering security group, granting read and write access to the CUI tree on SYS-003 and to the Arena PLM. The VPN has no MFA. The password was last set fourteen months ago.

Not known: who authenticated. Whether anything was accessed, opened or copied. Whether the sessions came from the former employee, from someone using credentials he left behind, or from an unrelated party. The FortiGate retains authentication events; SYS-003 and SYS-004 retain 30 days and are not reviewed, so file-level records for three of the four sessions have already rolled off.

Nakamura’s position: “It is almost certainly Jared. He was finishing a handover and he is a good guy. Nothing has been taken — we would see it.”

The determination

QuestionAnswer
Does it trigger?Yes. A cyber incident includes actions resulting in an actual or potentially adverse effect on an information system or the information residing in it. Unauthorised authentication to a network holding CUI, by an account that should not exist, with write access to the CUI store, is not merely potential — it is actual unauthorised access. The open question is only what was done with it.
Which paragraph252.204-7012(c)(1) for the rapid report; the definition of cyber incident at (a) supplies the threshold. Media preservation under (e) attaches once reported — urgently here, because logs are actively rolling off.
When the clock starts72 hours from discovery — Wednesday 09:40, when Nakamura identified the authentications. Not from the first session six weeks ago, and not from the separation date. Document the gap: a contracting officer will ask how an account authenticated four times unnoticed, and the answer is evidence about the monitoring capability rather than about the clock.
Who decidesNot Nakamura. There is an IRP naming an Incident Commander, and it is three years stale and lists a departed employee — so in practice the CISO and the CEO, who is also the Authorizing Official, must be involved. That the decision has nowhere to go is itself a finding under IR.L2-3.6.1.
What preservation now requiresImages of affected systems and relevant monitoring data for at least 90 days from the report. With 30-day retention and no review, the preservation obligation is already partly impossible to satisfy for the earlier sessions. Preserving what remains is urgent, and it is the first thing an assessor would ask whether anyone did.
The counter-argument, and the answer

Nakamura’s position deserves to be taken seriously before it is dismantled: it is almost certainly the former employee finishing a handover; there is no evidence of exfiltration; and reporting an incident involving a trusted ex-colleague feels disproportionate.

The threshold is “potentially adverse,” not “malicious,” and reporting is not an accusation. An authorised handover would have been performed on an authorised account. And “almost certainly Jared” is an inference about identity the environment cannot support: with no MFA, the authentication proves possession of a credential and nothing whatever about who held it.

His second claim — “nothing has been taken, we would see it” — is an appeal to the absence of evidence. Ask whether Meridian has the capability to have observed what he says has not happened. It does not: no file-access review, no SIEM, no correlation, and 30-day retention that has already consumed most of the record.

Could this organization know what it claims to know? That is the question you will ask for the rest of your career.

The Meridian case

Compute the score, then rule on the incident

The SPRS Score Calculator loads the thirteen gaps as Meridian maps them. Assign a point value to each, watch the running total, and see the eligibility threshold move against you. The tool checks each value against the methodology and flags the two over-deductions and the one trap that catch most people.

Then defend three deductions in writing, at least one of which must be a deduction you believe the company has mapped to the wrong requirement. State your mapping and why. Then say, in three or four sentences, what your score does and does not represent — be precise about what a gap list is, and about the difference between this figure and a certification determination.

Part B is INC-003, issued cold. Rule on it, name the clause paragraph, state when the clock starts and why, say who is entitled to make the call, and say what Meridian must hold in order to file at all. Then state the strongest argument against your decision and answer it. A decision that does not engage the other side is an opinion.

For faculty

Faculty teaching segment

  • Signature assignment: Students score a fictional gap list with the DoD Assessment Methodology (the D7 deliverable), defend three deductions in writing (one a remapped gap), explain what the score does and does not represent, and rule on an incident against the 72-hour reporting clock.
  • Rubric criteria:
    • Each deduction matches the methodology’s point value
    • At least one remapped gap is argued from the requirement text
    • The student distinguishes a self-assessment score from a certification determination
    • The incident ruling applies the reporting trigger correctly
  • Lab idea: Students score Meridian’s thirteen gaps in the SPRS Calculator; poll the room on the trickiest deduction before revealing.
  • Course fit: Compliance, risk metrics, incident response.
🎓 Teaching notePoll before revealing the remote-and-privileged deduction. Expect a substantial minority on the wrong value; the spread is the lesson.

Knowledge Check

Test your judgment

Ten questions in CCP style. Read every rationale.

Q1.MFA is enforced on the cloud tenant for all users, but not on the VPN (remote access) or on domain controller RDP (privileged access). What is deducted under 3.5.3?

Question 1 options
Correct: B. The exception is a conjunction, not a spectrum. Either one missing takes you to the full deduction. Why the others fall short: A Implemented somewhere is not implemented. The requirement reaches the assessment scope.; C The most common wrong answer here. Partial credit requires MFA for remote and privileged users. Neither is present.; D 3.5.3 is a 5-point requirement, and the partial-credit exception is the only variation available.

Q2.Encryption is employed to protect CUI but is not FIPS validated. What is deducted under 3.13.11?

Question 2 options
Correct: A. The second of the two partial-credit requirements. 5 if encryption is not employed at all. Note the interaction with POA&M rules: 3.13.11 is POA&M-eligible only where encryption is used but not FIPS validated. Why the others fall short: B Non-validated encryption does not satisfy the requirement.; C 5 applies where no encryption is employed. Here it is employed.; D The methodology contemplates exactly this state and prices it at 3.

Q3.Meridian has never submitted a SPRS score. Its gap list includes this as G-13, mapped to DFARS 252.204-7019. What is the deduction?

Question 3 options
Correct: A. Failing to submit a score is a contractual violation, not a scored one. Students who deduct for it have not understood what the instrument measures. Why the others fall short: B Significance is not the test. The methodology scores NIST SP 800-171 requirements.; C Still not a NIST requirement, so no point value attaches at any level.; D A missing prior score does not invalidate an assessment.

Q4.A contractor implements nothing at all. What score does the DoD Assessment Methodology produce?

Question 4 options
Correct: B. The weighted deductions total far more than 110. A negative score is not a computational error, and contractors do post them. Why the others fall short: A A natural guess, and the scale does not floor at zero.; C That would be the arithmetic if every requirement were worth 1 point. They are not.; D It is well defined, and −203 is the stated floor.

Q5.A stale VPN account belonging to a separated employee is found to have authenticated four times over six weeks; the most recent session was eleven days ago and the discovery is made Wednesday at 09:40. When does the 72-hour clock start?

Question 5 options
Correct: C. 72 hours from discovery. Document the six-week gap: a contracting officer will ask how an account authenticated four times unnoticed, and the answer speaks to the monitoring capability rather than to the deadline. Why the others fall short: A The clause runs from discovery, not from occurrence. The six-week gap belongs in the timeline as evidence about monitoring, and it does not move the clock.; B Confirmation is not the trigger. This is the reading contractors act on, and it is wrong — here it is also impossible, since most of the file-access record has already rolled off.; D The threshold is a potentially adverse effect. And with no MFA, the authentication proves possession of a credential and nothing about who held it.

Q6.Nakamura argues no report is needed because "nothing has been taken — we would see it." What is the assessor's strongest response?

Question 6 options
Correct: D. No PLM session logging, no log review, no defined retention. His position rests on an inference from absence of evidence, and absence of evidence proves nothing when nothing is looking. Why the others fall short: A Deferring past 72 hours is itself the violation, and the clock does not pause for an investigation.; B True, and it is a separate point. The substantive answer is that his claim is unsupportable on the organization's own capability.; C Overstated. The test is whether the incident meets the clause threshold, and here it does — but not because every lost device does.

Q7.What must Meridian hold in order to file a cyber incident report at all?

Question 7 options
Correct: C. Without it the report cannot be filed. Meridian has never obtained one, which means it will discover this at hour 70 of a 72-hour clock. Why the others fall short: A Necessary and not sufficient. One further item is required and Meridian has never obtained it.; B The rapid report precedes forensics. Waiting for analysis is how the clock is missed.; D No such authorisation is contemplated; the obligation runs to the contractor.

Q8.Which best distinguishes the DoD Assessment score from a CMMC certification determination?

Question 8 options
Correct: A. Different purposes, different mechanics, different consequences. A company can hold a respectable score and still fail certification — and two of the requirements that block certification are worth one point each. Why the others fall short: B Conflating them is the error this session exists to prevent.; C The DoD Assessment Methodology concerns the 110 NIST requirements, which is Level 2 territory.; D Reversed and wrong in both directions. The score is typically a contractor self-assessment; the certification determination is the C3PAO's.

Q9.A student computes Meridian's score as 59 and stops. What is missing?

Question 9 options
Correct: D. A real assessment examines all 110 and would find more NOT MET — media disposal, Dayton physical access, and the uncontrolled AWS upload path are three the list never reaches. Also worth stating: the score is not the only gate, because a POA&M-ineligible requirement is already failing. Why the others fall short: A It is the correct arithmetic and an incomplete answer. A bare number is what gets posted to SPRS in the real world.; B 59 ÷ 110 = 0.536 matters for the POA&M threshold, and it is arithmetic rather than the missing insight.; C No such benchmark governs anything in the methodology.

Q10.Marcus Ellison affirmed a score of 88 because "Priya and the consultant said that was the number." What is the assessor's finding?

Question 10 options
Correct: B. An affirmation that is not true is a false statement in a federal system with False Claims Act consequences for the signer. Nobody behaved dishonestly, the number is still wrong, and she is still the person who attested to it. Why the others fall short: A Reliance is not the standard. The affirmation is a present-tense attestation of continued compliance by someone with authority to bind the organization.; C A consultant cannot bind the OSC and does not carry the affirmation exposure.; D Supplying a wrong number is a contributing failure. The attestation, and its exposure, sit with the signer.
